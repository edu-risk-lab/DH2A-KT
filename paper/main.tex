\begin{abstract}
Graph-augmented knowledge tracing (KT) often lacks leakage audits of its
auxiliary graphs and rarely exposes frozen predictions to a gated
explanation layer. We present DH$^2$A-KT, a two-tier system that is
tractable on a single consumer GPU. \textbf{Tier~1 (DH$^2$-KT)} reuses the
leakage-controlled, train-only concept-graph protocol of companion paper
P0, groups audited prerequisite edges into \emph{concept-prerequisite
chain hyperedges} (the quantitative core evaluated here), and extends
P0's diagnostics with hyperedge-level leakage checks. Discussion-thread and
teacher-intervention types remain data-blocked; session+Hint hyperedges and
a limited observational hint ATE are demonstrated on FoundationalASSIST
(not on XES3G5M). \textbf{Tier~2 (AgentDG-KT)} is a small-scale pilot: Diagnostician,
Critic, and Tutor agents read Tier~1's \emph{frozen} outputs. On XES3G5M, DH$^2$-KT
v2 (chain hyperedges, graph-only interaction) achieves mean test AUC
0.752 versus reused GKT 0.834 under matched budget, while passing a
hypergraph manipulation check ($\Delta$AUC$=0.038$ at $p{=}0.9$
node-drop); P0 GKT also passes destruction at the same anchor with a
larger mean drop, so reliance is not unique to DH$^2$-KT. Junyi is used only for structural ground-truth overlap (not
full model training). A 500-sample Tier~2 pilot with Qwen2.5-7B (Ollama)
yields Critic flag rates of 12.4\% under a strict prompt and 0\% after
probability-format calibration; a 40-sample dual-rater study rates
faithfulness $4.01{\pm}1.07$ and usefulness $3.53{\pm}0.71$ (Likert 1--5).
FoundationalASSIST supplies session+Hint hyperedges and a limited
observational hint ATE (Section~\ref{sec:foundational-assist}).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% =============================================================================

Knowledge tracing (KT) estimates a learner's latent mastery of knowledge
components (KCs) from interaction history, supporting adaptive practice,
intelligent tutoring, and curriculum recommendation. The field has
progressed from probabilistic and sequence models
\cite{piech2015dkt} to graph-augmented models that propagate information
over auxiliary concept graphs \cite{nakagawa2019gkt,yang2021gikt}, and, most
recently, toward hypergraph representations that capture higher-order
relations among knowledge components \cite{thmn2025,hgkt2025,htkt2025} and
dynamic, temporally-evolving graph structure \cite{dygkt2024}.

This paper builds on -- rather than replacing -- \cite{daominh2026p0}'s
leakage-control protocol. Empirically, Tier~1 evaluates
\emph{concept-prerequisite} chain hyperedges under a graph-only encoder
and a manipulation check. Discussion-thread and teacher-intervention
hyperedges remain data-blocked; only session+Hint hyperedges and a limited
observational hint ATE are demonstrated on FoundationalASSIST (weak
propensity overlap; Sections~\ref{sec:tier1-graph}--\ref{sec:causal}).
Section~\ref{sec:leakage-ext} extends P0's diagnostics to the hyperedge
setting. Tier~2 (Section~\ref{sec:tier2}) is a deliberately-scoped pilot
showing that an LLM-agent layer can read Tier~1's frozen output without
reopening the leakage question. On XES3G5M, graph-only interaction trades
$\approx$8.2 AUC points versus GKT for a passing manipulation check;
Tier~2 faithfulness at pilot scale is sensitive to Critic prompt design
but reaches 0\% flag rate after calibration (Section~\ref{sec:results}).
Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture} summarizes the two-tier pipeline.

\subsection{Motivation}
Three concrete gaps motivate this work:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Higher-order structure is under-audited in graph-KT.}
  Hypergraph-KT models \cite{thmn2025,hgkt2025,htkt2025} group multiple
  knowledge components into a single hyperedge, yet published systems
  rarely audit whether those hyperedges were constructed without leakage,
  and few expose an interface for entities beyond KCs (hints, teachers,
  forums, video) when such logs become available.
  \item \textbf{Graph provenance is rarely audited.} \cite{daominh2026p0}
  shows that pooling a knowledge-component graph from the full interaction
  log before a learner-based train/test split lets held-out information
  reach a graph-augmented KT model even when the sequence optimizer itself
  trains only on the training fold. \cite{daominh2026p0}'s protocol audits
  this channel for \emph{pairwise} prerequisite/similarity edges; no
  published audit protocol currently covers \emph{hyperedges}.
  \item \textbf{Causal claims about pedagogical intervention are rare.} Most
  graph-KT models report predictive accuracy only, without attempting to
  separate a hint or teacher action's causal effect on subsequent
  correctness from confounded correlation --- motivating an interface for
  ATE estimation even when public logs do not yet support reporting numbers.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Contributions}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{C1.} A hypergraph construction procedure
  (Section~\ref{sec:tier1-graph}) that groups \cite{daominh2026p0}'s
  audited, acyclic prerequisite edges into concept-prerequisite chain
  hyperedges (\emph{evaluated quantitatively} on XES3G5M/Junyi), plus
  session hyperedges with Hint members on FoundationalASSIST
  \cite{foundationalassist2026} (Section~\ref{sec:foundational-assist});
  discussion-thread and teacher-intervention types remain data-blocked
  (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).
  \item \textbf{C2.} A hyperedge-level extension of \cite{daominh2026p0}'s
  four-diagnostic leakage taxonomy, adding three classes --
  group-membership, cross-modal, and intervention-timing leakage -- that a
  pairwise audit structurally cannot detect (Section~\ref{sec:leakage-ext}).
  \item \textbf{C3.} A causal-effect layer
  (Section~\ref{sec:causal}) implementing inverse-propensity ATE estimation;
  on FoundationalASSIST we report a \emph{limited} observational hint ATE
  (next-step correctness) with propensity diagnostics
  (Section~\ref{sec:foundational-assist}), while XES3G5M still has no
  synchronized hint treatment for ATE.
  \item \textbf{C4.} Direct reuse of \cite{daominh2026p0}'s published,
  leakage-controlled baseline results (BKT, DKT, AKT, simpleKT, GKT, GIKT,
  SKT, DyGKT, DGEKT) as the Tier~1 comparison column under matched
  protocol and split seeds, avoiding redundant baseline retraining.
  \item \textbf{C5.} A Tier~2 pilot (AgentDG-KT, Section~\ref{sec:tier2})
  showing that a Critic-gated LLM interpretability loop over frozen Tier~1
  outputs is tractable at $n{=}500$ on a consumer GPU, plus a dual-rater
  Likert study ($n{=}40$) of faithfulness/usefulness
  (Table~\ref{tab:human-eval}) --- scoped as pilot evidence, not a full
  classroom RCT.
  \item \textbf{C6.} Empirical evidence on XES3G5M that (i) a graph-only
  DH$^2$-KT backbone passes a hypergraph manipulation check
  ($\Delta$AUC$=0.038$ on fold~0) while remaining below reused GKT AUC
  (mean $0.752{\pm}0.002$ vs.\ $0.834{\pm}0.001$), noting that P0 GKT
  \emph{also} passes destruction at $p{=}0.9$ with a larger mean drop, and
  (ii) the local 7B Tier~2 pilot reaches 0\% Critic flag rate after
  probability-format calibration (Section~\ref{sec:results}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{Graph- and hypergraph-based knowledge tracing}
Early graph-augmented KT models propagate information over a single,
pairwise concept graph: \cite{nakagawa2019gkt} layer a graph neural network
over a knowledge-component graph on top of a sequence model, and
\cite{yang2021gikt} let exercises and concepts recursively re-rank one
another through a bipartite exercise-concept graph. \cite{dygkt2024} extends
this line to a temporally-evolving graph that updates edges as new
interactions arrive rather than freezing the graph after construction. A
second line moves from pairwise edges to hyperedges spanning more than two
knowledge components at once: \cite{thmn2025} proposes a temporal hypergraph
memory network, \cite{hgkt2025} adds a dual-gated mechanism controlling how
much a hyperedge's aggregated signal influences a given prediction, and
\cite{htkt2025} replaces graph convolution with a hypergraph transformer. A
third line adds heterogeneity in what a node \emph{represents} rather than
how many nodes an edge spans: \cite{psykt2024} augments the concept graph
with psychological-factor nodes, and \cite{hhskt2023} builds a heterogeneous
graph directly over learner-question interactions. Across all three lines,
every published hyperedge or heterogeneous node we are aware of is still
drawn from a closed set of KC-, exercise-, or learner-adjacent entities; none
represent a Teacher intervention, Hint content, Forum discussion, or
instructional Video as a first-class member inside the same hyperedge as the
knowledge components it plausibly affects -- the interface gap C1
(Section~\ref{sec:tier1-graph}) targets, while this paper's quantitative
core evaluates concept-prerequisite chains.

\subsection{LLM agents in education and graph-augmented LLM agents}
\cite{llmedu2025} surveys the growing use of LLM agents across tutoring,
feedback generation, and curriculum planning in education, and
\cite{agentcat2026} instantiates a multi-agent LLM pipeline that simulates
computerized adaptive testing end to end, with agents standing in for both
the test administrator and the examinee. Outside education,
\cite{graphaugmentedllm2025} surveys how LLM agents can read from and write
to an external graph as working memory, and \cite{graphrag2025} applies a
graph-retrieval-augmented-generation pattern to recommend personalized
learning paths over a dual knowledge-structure graph. AgentDG-KT
(Section~\ref{sec:tier2}) differs from this pattern in one specific way:
none of the above agents are gated by a design in which the graph -- and the
quantitative model that reads it -- is frozen before the agent acts, with
the agent limited to producing a natural-language artifact (rationale,
hint, session hyperedge) that a separate Critic agent must approve before it
is treated as anything more than a proposal. We adopt this
frozen-core-plus-pilot-agent split specifically because a full multi-agent
LLM pipeline that re-infers knowledge state end to end over millions of
interactions is not tractable on a single consumer GPU
(Section~\ref{sec:tier2}), not because we believe agentic re-inference is
undesirable in principle.

\subsection{Leakage-controlled evaluation in knowledge tracing}
\cite{daominh2026p0} is the companion paper this work builds on and reuses
directly (Section~\ref{sec:reuse}); we summarize its protocol here so the
rest of this paper is self-contained. \cite{daominh2026p0} observes that a
knowledge-component graph is often constructed by pooling co-occurrence or
similarity statistics over the \emph{entire} interaction log before a
learner-based train/test split is applied, which lets held-out information
reach a graph-augmented KT model through the graph channel even when the
sequence model itself trains only on the training fold. Its audit protocol
reports four scalar diagnostics computed on a train-only-constructed graph
-- an edge-composition-ratio flag ($\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{flag}}$), an
edge-composition overlap ratio ($\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{overlap}}$), an
edge-outcome correlation ($|\rho|$), and a train/test boundary-mismatch rate
($\mathrm{TBMR}$) -- alongside a DAG audit with cycle pruning, a cold-start
stratification of knowledge components into four frequency strata, a DAG
Disruption Rate (DDR) manipulation check under five structured augmentation
operators, and a ground-truth cross-validation against an expert-curated
prerequisite DAG on Junyi Academy. Across XES3G5M, ASSISTments~2012, and
Junyi Academy, \cite{daominh2026p0} reports that train-only graph
construction shifts baseline AUC by at most 0.003 relative to full-log
construction -- evidence that leakage control, in their setting, does not by
itself manufacture the reported gains, and a result we rely on directly when
interpreting any Tier~1 gain in this paper (Section~\ref{sec:reuse}).
Section~\ref{sec:leakage-ext} extends this four-diagnostic protocol from
pairwise edges to heterogeneous hyperedges.

\subsection{Causal inference in educational data mining}
Causal analysis in education has mostly targeted two separate questions:
estimating the effect of a known treatment, and discovering an unknown
causal structure among skills. \cite{kaur2019causal} addresses the first:
given historical course-enrollment and grade data, they estimate the
average treatment effect of taking one course before another using matching
and regression -- directly analogous in spirit to the propensity-score
estimator we use for hint/teacher-intervention effects
(Section~\ref{sec:causal}), though on a coarser course-level treatment
rather than a step-level pedagogical action. \cite{kumar2023causal}
addresses the second: they learn a latent causal ordering and dependency
structure among knowledge-component skills end to end inside a modified DKT
model, evaluated on the NeurIPS 2022 Challenge on Causal Insights for
Learning Paths in Education. \cite{minn2022interpretable} sits between the
two, using a causal-relations-constrained student model to make KT
predictions more interpretable without a separate treatment-effect
estimation step. Our causal-effect layer (Section~\ref{sec:causal}) is
closest in spirit to \cite{kaur2019causal} -- a treatment-effect estimator
rather than a causal-discovery method -- but applies it at the level of
individual hint/teacher-intervention hyperedges rather than curriculum-level
course sequencing, and explicitly reports the propensity-overlap diagnostic
that \cite{kaur2019causal} does not discuss, following the
identification-assumption caveat in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

\section{Preliminaries}
% =============================================================================

Let $\mathcal{D} = \{(u, t, q, c, y)\}$ be a KT interaction log with learner
$u$, timestamp $t$, item $q$, knowledge component $c$, and correctness label
$y \in \{0, 1\}$, following \cite{daominh2026p0}'s notation. A learner-based
temporal split $\mathcal{F} = (\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{train}},
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{val}}, \mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{test}})$ assigns each
learner to exactly one fold (ratios $0.7/0.1/0.2$, three folds with split
seeds $42$/$43$/$44$, matching \cite{daominh2026p0}'s protocol so results
remain directly comparable, see Section~\ref{sec:reuse}).

\subsection{Self-contained summary of the reused P0 protocol}
\label{sec:p0-selfcontained}
Because \cite{daominh2026p0} is under review at the time of writing, we
summarize the reused pieces so this manuscript can be checked without
reading the companion paper in full. \textbf{Construction.} For each
learner-based fold, prerequisite edges $E_{\mathrm{pre}}^f$ and similarity
edges $E_{\mathrm{sim}}^f$ are built from \emph{train-fold interactions
only}, then audited for cycles (DAG pruning). \textbf{Four pairwise
diagnostics} (reported on the train-only graph against held-out learners):
edge-composition-ratio flag $\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{flag}}$, overlap
$\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{overlap}}$, edge--outcome correlation $|\rho|$, and
train/test boundary-mismatch rate $\mathrm{TBMR}$. Across XES3G5M,
ASSISTments~2012, and Junyi, \cite{daominh2026p0} finds that switching from
full-log to train-only graph construction changes baseline AUC by at most
0.003 --- evidence that leakage control alone does not manufacture large
gains. \textbf{Reuse artefacts.} We pin the companion repository to commit
\texttt{5e3d6a01c32a649f1655ac8712f03359bddfe478} and copy its published
baseline CSV tables verbatim for the nine models listed in
Section~\ref{sec:reuse}; config checksum verification for XES3G5M is
reported there. Concept-prerequisite hyperedges in this paper are formed by
enumerating bounded-length paths on the same audited $E_{\mathrm{pre}}^f$
(Section~\ref{sec:tier1-graph}).

We extend this notation with a \emph{hyperedge} $h = (\kappa,
\{(\tau_i, e_i)\}_{i=1}^{k}, f)$: a kind label $\kappa \in
\{\text{concept-prerequisite}, \text{session}, \text{discussion-thread},
\text{teacher-intervention}\}$, a set of $k$ typed member entities
$(\tau_i, e_i)$ with entity type $\tau_i \in \{\text{student, exercise,
concept, teacher, hint, forum, discussion, video}\}$, and a fold identifier
$f$. A heterogeneous hypergraph $\mathcal{H}_f = (\mathcal{V}_f,
\mathcal{E}_f^{\mathrm{hyper}})$ for fold $f$ is a set of such hyperedges
over the typed node set $\mathcal{V}_f$. \textbf{Scope of this paper:}
only $\kappa=\text{concept-prerequisite}$ hyperedges (members are concept
nodes along audited $E_{\mathrm{pre}}$ paths) enter Tier~1 training and
the quantitative tables below; the other three kinds are future-proof
interfaces plus Tier~2 session write-backs at pilot scale
(Section~\ref{sec:tier1-graph}).

\subsection{Design goal}
For each fold $f$, the goal is a hypergraph $\mathcal{H}_f$ constructed
using only $\mathcal{F}_f^{\mathrm{train}}$ evidence, audited for the
leakage classes in Table~\ref{tab:leakage-taxonomy}
(Section~\ref{sec:leakage-ext}), that a downstream Tier~1 encoder consumes
to predict $y$ for held-out interactions.

\subsection{Heterogeneous hypergraph construction}
\label{sec:tier1-graph}

Concept-prerequisite hyperedges are built by enumerating simple directed
paths of bounded length on \cite{daominh2026p0}'s audited, acyclic
$E_{\mathrm{pre}}^f$ (Algorithm~1 in code: \texttt{build\_concept\_prerequisite\_hyperedges}).

\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE \textbf{Input:} audited, acyclic prerequisite edges
$E_{\mathrm{pre}}^f$ from \cite{daominh2026p0}'s Algorithm~2; chain length
bounds $[\ell_{\min}, \ell_{\max}]$
\STATE \textbf{Output:} concept-prerequisite hyperedge set
$\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{cprereq}}_f$
\STATE Build directed graph $G$ from $E_{\mathrm{pre}}^f$
\FOR{each root $r$ with in-degree 0 in $G$}
  \FOR{each simple path $p$ from $r$ with $\ell_{\min} \le |p| \le
  \ell_{\max}$}
    \STATE emit hyperedge with members $\{(\text{concept}, c) : c \in p\}$
  \ENDFOR
\ENDFOR
\end{algorithmic}
\label{alg:concept-hyperedge}

Discussion-thread and teacher-intervention hyperedges remain
\emph{data-blocked}: no synchronized Forum or Teacher-action logs ship with
the public KT corpora we use. Session hyperedges
(Student-Exercise-Concept-Hint) are constructed on
FoundationalASSIST \cite{foundationalassist2026}, which provides
\texttt{hint\_count} synchronized to each interaction
(Section~\ref{sec:foundational-assist}). On the three P0 core benchmarks
(XES3G5M, ASSISTments~2012, Junyi), session construction stays blocked
because those logs lack Hint/Video columns; ES-KT-24 \cite{esk2024} remains
an optional Video-bearing source for future work. Tier~1's primary
predictive core on XES3G5M therefore still uses concept-prerequisite
hyperedges; FoundationalASSIST supplies the heterogeneous session+Hint
evidence and a secondary predictive fold.

\subsection{Hyperedge-level leakage audit}
\label{sec:leakage-ext}

\cite{daominh2026p0} audits five leakage classes at the level of pairwise
edge provenance (structural, edge, target, temporal, cold-start
neighbourhood) using four scalar diagnostics: $\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{flag}}$,
$\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{overlap}}$, $|\rho|$, and $\mathrm{TBMR}$. These
diagnostics are reused unmodified (Section~\ref{sec:reuse}) on the pairwise
projection of a hyperedge set. Table~\ref{tab:leakage-taxonomy} adds three
classes that a pairwise audit cannot express because they require a
\emph{group} of heterogeneous members, not a single edge.

\subsection{Encoding and hypergraph attention}
DH$^2$-KT applies two \texttt{HypergraphConv} layers per hyperedge kind on
concept embeddings, mean-pools across kinds, and masks concepts not
participating in any hyperedge. Sequence encoding uses a \emph{graph-only}
interaction term $x_t = \mathrm{proj}(c_t) + \mathrm{proj}(r_t)$ with a
learnable graph gain on $c_t$, deliberately omitting direct exercise
embeddings so predictions cannot bypass the hypergraph (Section~\ref{sec:results}).
A dual-gated update combines $x_t$ with the previous hidden state; a linear
head outputs next-step logits. Training adds a graph-sensitivity auxiliary
loss encouraging concept states to depend on hypergraph structure.

\subsection{Causal-effect layer}
\label{sec:causal}
We estimate the average treatment effect (ATE) of a binary intervention
(e.g., hint given) on next-step correctness via inverse-propensity
weighting, following the identification assumption that intervention
assignment is unconfounded given observed features -- a strong assumption on
observational KT data that we report as a limitation rather than a proven
property (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). Given confounders $X$, treatment
$T \in \{0,1\}$, and outcome $Y$, the propensity score $\hat{e}(X) =
P(T=1\mid X)$ is estimated by logistic regression, and
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\mathrm{ATE}} = \frac{\sum_i T_i Y_i / \hat{e}(X_i)}{\sum_i T_i /
\hat{e}(X_i)} - \frac{\sum_i (1-T_i) Y_i / (1-\hat{e}(X_i))}{\sum_i (1-T_i) /
(1-\hat{e}(X_i))}.
\label{eq:ate}
\end{equation}
No ATE numbers are reported on the primary XES3G5M benchmark (no hint
logs). On FoundationalASSIST we estimate an observational IPW ATE of
\texttt{hint\_used}$_t$ on \texttt{correct}$_{t+1}$ with pre-$t$ summaries
as confounders (Section~\ref{sec:foundational-assist}); same-step
\texttt{discrete\_score} is \emph{not} used as the outcome because it is
mechanically tied to hint requests in that corpus.

\section{Tier 2: AgentDG-KT pilot}
\label{sec:tier2}
% =============================================================================

Tier~2 is deliberately scoped as a pilot, not a benchmark at Tier~1's
quantitative rigor (docs/idea-D-plan.md, following the feasibility analysis
in the accompanying project discussion: a full multi-agent LLM re-inference
pipeline over millions of interactions is not tractable on a single
consumer GPU within a realistic project timeline). Three agents operate on
Tier~1's \emph{frozen} output rather than re-inferring knowledge state:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Diagnostician Agent} produces a natural-language rationale
  for a given Tier-1 prediction, explicitly instructed not to override the
  given probability.
  \item \textbf{Tutor/Hint Agent} proposes the next hint/exercise and writes
  a new session hyperedge back into the graph (the agentic closed loop).
  \item \textbf{Critic Agent} is a \emph{mandatory} quality gate that flags
  any Diagnostician output contradicting or ignoring the Tier-1 number it
  was meant to explain, before anything is treated as ground truth in a
  case study.
\end{itemize}

We ran a 500-sample pilot on XES3G5M fold~0 (seed 42) with Tier~1 frozen
(checkpoint from graph-only v2 training) and Qwen2.5-7B (Ollama 0.32.6).
Table~\ref{tab:tier2} reports Critic flag rates; v1's 12.4\% flags were
predominantly probability \emph{format} mismatches (e.g., ``69.3\%''
vs.\ $P{=}0.693$), resolved in v2 by \texttt{critic\_calibration.py} and an
updated Critic prompt. The Tutor agent wrote 500 session hyperedges back to
the graph, closing the agentic loop at pilot scale.

\subsection{Datasets}
\label{sec:reuse}
Following \cite{daominh2026p0}'s benchmark-role assignment, XES3G5M
\cite{liu2023xes3g5m} (18{,}066 learners, 7{,}652 items, 865 KCs,
6{,}413{,}353 interactions) is the \emph{primary} predictive benchmark:
low consecutive-KC-repeat rate, non-saturated headline AUC, and the
clearest graph-backbone separation among the corpora \cite{daominh2026p0}
studied. All DH$^2$-KT training and the manipulation check reported below
use XES3G5M. ASSISTments~2012 is listed as secondary in the P0 protocol
(single-skill Q-matrix); we do \emph{not} reuse P0 ASSISTments~2012 GKT AUC
as a comparison column. Instead we report a secondary fold on the newer
FoundationalASSIST corpus \cite{foundationalassist2026}
(Section~\ref{sec:foundational-assist}), which is \emph{not}
interchangeable with classic ASSISTments~2012. Junyi Academy supplies the
expert prerequisite DAG for \emph{structural} ground-truth overlap only
(Section~\ref{sec:junyi-gt}); full DH$^2$-KT training on Junyi is not run.

\subsection{Baselines reused from P0}
\label{sec:baselines-reuse}
The protocol summary in Section~\ref{sec:p0-selfcontained} underpins reuse.
We reuse \cite{daominh2026p0}'s published baseline results
(\texttt{baselines/p0\_reused/baseline\_results.csv}, 69 rows, and

\subsection{Manipulation check}
Following \cite{daominh2026p0}'s Section~4.7 reasoning, we do not interpret
any ablation result as evidence of hypergraph benefit unless DH$^2$-KT first
passes a manipulation check: AUC must move meaningfully under near-total
($p=0.90$) destruction of the constructed hyperedge set. A backbone whose
AUC does not move under total destruction is \emph{graph-inert}, and its
ablation numbers carry no interpretable meaning about graph benefit.
On XES3G5M fold~0, DH$^2$-KT v2 passes this gate
($\Delta$AUC$=0.038$; Table~\ref{tab:manipulation}); an earlier graph-inert
architecture (pairwise edges + exercise embedding path) failed with
$\Delta$AUC $\approx 0.0003$, motivating the graph-only redesign.

\subsection{Predictive performance (RQ1)}

\subsection{Manipulation check (RQ2)}
\label{sec:manipulation-result}
Table~\ref{tab:manipulation} and Fig.~\ref{fig:manipulation-sweep} summarize
graph-destruction evidence at the shared P0 anchor ($p{=}0.9$ node-drop).
DH$^2$-KT v2 (fold~0) loses $\Delta$AUC$=0.038$ when frozen weights are
re-evaluated on a destroyed hypergraph, confirming hypergraph use under our
protocol (an earlier pairwise$+$exercise-embedding backbone failed this gate,
$\Delta$AUC$\approx 0.0003$). Reused P0 DDR-downstream results show that
\textbf{GKT also passes} the same operator/strength on XES3G5M, with a
\emph{larger} mean drop ($0.0877{\pm}0.0009$, $n{=}9$ fold--seed runs) after
retraining on the destroyed pairwise graph. Protocol differences matter
(DH$^2$-KT freezes weights; P0 GKT retrains), but either way graph-reliance
is \emph{not} unique to DH$^2$-KT. simpleKT has no concept-graph adjacency,
so $\Delta$AUC under graph destruction is undefined (N/A).
Fig.~\ref{fig:manipulation-sweep} draws an \emph{illustrative} horizontal
line at $\Delta$AUC$=0.003$: roughly an order of magnitude above the
observed graph-inert noise floor ($\approx 3{\times}10^{-4}$), not a formal
hypothesis test from \cite{daominh2026p0}. We treat a backbone as
graph-reliant when its destruction $\Delta$AUC clearly exceeds that floor
(here $0.038$ for DH$^2$-KT; $\approx 0.088$ for GKT).

\subsection{Ground-truth structural overlap (Junyi)}
\label{sec:junyi-gt}
Table~\ref{tab:junyi-gt} compares hyperedge-chain overlap with P0's pairwise

\subsection{Secondary corpus: FoundationalASSIST (session+Hint)}
\label{sec:foundational-assist}
FoundationalASSIST \cite{foundationalassist2026} provides
$\approx$1.74M skill-linked interactions with synchronized

train-only $E_{\mathrm{pre}}$ (280 edges after cycle pruning), build

\section{Discussion}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{Threats to validity}
Our XES3G5M comparison uses \cite{daominh2026p0}'s reused baselines under
matched batch/epoch budget (Table~\ref{tab:auc-xes3g5m}); preprocessing
configs are pinned to P0's vendored commit. DH$^2$-KT mean AUC is
$0.7516{\pm}0.0015$ vs.\ GKT $0.8336{\pm}0.0010$ (paired
$\Delta{=}{-}0.082{\pm}0.003$ over three folds). Both DH$^2$-KT and GKT
pass strong graph-destruction checks; the AUC gap therefore cannot be read
as ``DH$^2$-KT uniquely buys graph-reliance.'' Section~\ref{sec:auc-tradeoff}
repositions value around audit, heterogeneous/causal interfaces, and Tier~2
rather than exclusive graph sensitivity. Primary claims remain scoped to
concept-prerequisite hyperedges on XES3G5M; FoundationalASSIST is secondary
(Section~\ref{sec:foundational-assist}). Passing destruction confirms graph
use for a backbone, not that a specific hyperedge kind drives predictive gain.

\subsection{What justifies DH$^2$-KT if graph-reliance is not unique?}
\label{sec:auc-tradeoff}
Because GKT also passes destruction while beating DH$^2$-KT on clean AUC
(Table~\ref{tab:manipulation}), the remaining case for DH$^2$-KT is what
reused GKT does \emph{not} provide: (i)~hyperedge-level leakage diagnostics
beyond pairwise $E_{\mathrm{pre}}$ (Table~\ref{tab:leakage-taxonomy});
(ii)~a shared schema for heterogeneous members (session/Hint/teacher/forum/
video), exercised for session+Hint on FoundationalASSIST while
discussion-thread and teacher types stay data-blocked;
(iii)~an inverse-propensity ATE layer for pedagogical interventions
(Section~\ref{sec:causal}; limited observational hint ATE on
FoundationalASSIST); and (iv)~a Critic-gated explanation loop over frozen
$P(\mathrm{correct})$ (Section~\ref{sec:tier2}). Protocol note: DH$^2$-KT
$\Delta$AUC is frozen-weight re-evaluation; P0 GKT $\Delta$AUC retrains on
the destroyed pairwise graph --- complementary, not apple-to-apple.
\textbf{Prefer high-AUC baselines} when ranking alone is the product goal;
\textbf{prefer DH$^2$-KT} when stakeholders need higher-order auditability,
an extensible heterogeneous/causal interface, and/or Critic-gated
explanations, accepting the $\approx$8~point AUC gap versus GKT
($-0.082{\pm}0.003$; Table~\ref{tab:auc-xes3g5m}) as the cost of our
graph-only path --- not a claim that GKT is graph-inert.

\subsection{Tier~2 faithfulness}
Tier~2's 12.4\% v1 flag rate was largely a Critic prompt artifact
(\% vs.\ decimal); calibration reduced it to 0\% on 500 samples without
changing Tier~1 numbers. Dual-rater scores (Table~\ref{tab:human-eval})
place faithfulness near ``aligned'' ($\approx$4/5) and usefulness in the
``somewhat--mostly useful'' band ($\approx$3.5/5), with high within-$\pm$1
agreement; usefulness Spearman is moderate, reflecting more subjective
teacher-facing judgments.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Discussion-thread and teacher-intervention hyperedges remain
  data-blocked; session+Hint hyperedges are demonstrated on
  FoundationalASSIST (concept projection; $+0.002$ AUC vs.\ concept-only)
  but are not the primary XES3G5M training graph.
  \item The causal-effect layer's identification assumption (no unmeasured
  confounding given observed features) is stated, not proven; the
  FoundationalASSIST hint ATE shows weak propensity overlap and should not
  be read as a recommendation against hints.
  \item The hyperedge leakage audit's pairwise-projection approximation
  (Section~\ref{sec:leakage-ext}) weights all projected pairs equally; a
  hyperedge-native statistic is future work.
  \item Tier~2 pilot: one fold, one local 7B LLM; Critic sensitivity to
  response format required calibration; human ratings use $n{=}40$ and
  two raters (no third rater; not a classroom deployment study).
  \item Reproduction environment: Windows workstation, single RTX~3090;
  42/42 unit tests pass in the released codebase.
\end{itemize}

% =============================================================================
\section{Conclusion and future work}
% =============================================================================
We presented DH$^2$A-KT, a two-tier framework combining leakage-controlled
hypergraph knowledge tracing (DH$^2$-KT) with a frozen-core LLM agent pilot.
On XES3G5M, graph-only DH$^2$-KT and reused GKT both pass strong
destruction checks; DH$^2$-KT remains below GKT AUC
($0.752{\pm}0.002$ vs.\ $0.834{\pm}0.001$) under a graph-only interaction
path. Quantitative claims are scoped to concept-prerequisite chain
hyperedges. On Junyi, those hyperedges
show structural overlap with the expert DAG (edge-F1 $\approx 0.17$) without
full-model training. Tier~2 demonstrates a Critic-gated loop at 500-sample
scale with 0\% flag rate after calibration and dual-rater Likert means of
$\approx$4.0 / 3.5 for faithfulness / usefulness. On FoundationalASSIST we add
session+Hint hyperedges, a secondary fold-0 AUC, and a limited
observational hint ATE with propensity diagnostics. Future work includes
richer confounders / sensitivity analysis for ATE, a third rater or larger
human sample, ES-KT-24 Video wiring, and hyperedge-native leakage
statistics.
