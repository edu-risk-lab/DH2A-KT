\begin{abstract}
Knowledge tracing systems add auxiliary knowledge, such as concept
graphs or response timing, to a sequence model and are usually judged
by one area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC),
which hides which component earned the gain; knowledge built from the
full log can also leak held-out learners into training. We propose a
credit protocol that builds auxiliary knowledge from training learners
only, audits membership and input availability, compares each component
with a control declared in advance, and assigns one of three outcomes
from ten paired seeds under Holm (family-wise error) or
Benjamini--Hochberg (false-discovery rate) adjustment on pooled
held-out evaluation AUC.
Unlike ablations that remove an input together with its weights, the
protocol separates the input from the weights that read it. We apply it
to one elapsed-time component on three corpora, two encoders, and three
learner folds as a reanalysis of collected ten-seed runs under a rule
fixed after an earlier interval/seed map was known; it is not a
confirmatory test on unused data. Only the full time package on XES3G5M
with a recurrent tracer is credited; the aligned gap input alone stays
positive and is not credited. Nothing is credited with an attention
encoder or on ASSISTments~2012. On Junyi, gaps that are not available at
prediction time are reported as leakage diagnostics rather than credit.
In a simulation, a hint chosen with knowledge of the next response
raises test AUC by $0.00518$ and would be labelled credited under the
earlier five-seed interval/seed rules; that calculation is a family~D
diagnostic, not the primary ten-seed Holm rule, and only a provenance
audit flags it. Compared with single-AUC comparisons, the protocol
states which component, control, and input availability a reported gain
rests on.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% =============================================================================

Knowledge tracing (KT) estimates a learner's latent mastery from an
interaction history and predicts the next response, so that a tutor
can choose the next item \cite{corbett1995bkt}. Modern tracers are built in a
knowledge-engineering fashion: knowledge is acquired from the log or
from experts, represented as question--concept incidence, prerequisite
graphs, hyperedges, or timing features, and injected into a sequence
model \cite{yang2021gikt,nakagawa2019gkt,shen2021lpkt}. The resulting
system is then usually validated by one area under the receiver
operating characteristic curve (AUC) for the whole model.

That validation step is weak in two ways. First, one AUC does not say
which added piece of knowledge earned the gain. A new branch adds both
an input and parameters, and a gain of a few thousandths of AUC can only
be separated from training noise with paired seeds and a declared
threshold \cite{bouthillier2021variance}. Second, auxiliary knowledge built from
the full log can carry held-out learners into training, which is one
of the leakage types that inflate reported results across
machine-learning-based science \cite{kapoor2023leakage}. Re-evaluations
outside KT have found that published neural and graph-based gains often
shrink or vanish under fair comparison
\cite{ferraridacrema2019progress,shehzad2025gnnsbrs}. Inside KT, which
model family leads depends on the dataset \cite{gervet2020deepkt}, and
gains from attention-augmented models shrink under a controlled
protocol \cite{kim2026pktreliability}; exports that repeat an item
once per knowledge component (KC), known as KC-expanded exports, can
themselves leak labels \cite{badran2025labelleak,liu2022pykt}. These
studies ask whether one model beats another under a fair protocol. The
question for a knowledge engineer is narrower: which piece of the
injected knowledge may be credited, against what control, and does that
decision hold on another corpus or backbone?

This paper proposes a credit protocol for that decision
(Section~\ref{sec:protocol}). Auxiliary knowledge is built from training
learners only, audited for membership leakage, and compared with a
control declared before the run. Each comparison receives one of three
outcomes.

When the protocol is applied to one elapsed-time message on two
encoders, three corpora, and three learner folds, as a reanalysis of
the collected ten-seed runs, primary-family credit
decisions differ by setting (Table~\ref{tab:credit-primary-c}), though
ASSISTments package means remain close to the XES3G5M ones and are not
shown heterogeneous here. On XES3G5M with a recurrent tracer the time
package is credited on every learner fold, but only as a package: the
aligned gap stays positive and is not credited after Holm adjustment on
every fold (means about $+1.9$ to $+2.0\times 10^{-3}$), so a
package-level ablation overstates what the gap input contributes. That
package credit is for attempt-onset scoring with a query-side gap; a
tutor that must choose the next item before it opens cannot claim the
same package. On ASSISTments~2012 and under an attention encoder on
XES3G5M, no primary-family contrast is credited; the same attention
package on the ASSISTments one-second export is exploratory and
likewise not credited. Junyi completion-time query gaps look strong but
are family~D diagnostics: they include the duration of the scored item.
A history-only Junyi gap, which is available at attempt onset, is
positive on every seed and not credited (family~E), as is the
history-only package on XES3G5M. A rebuilt attempt-onset gap that moves
with post-response retries fails an availability check and is likewise
excluded from credit (Section~\ref{sec:junyi-onset-audit}). Nothing is
credited on a coarse timestamp export. A single-setting ablation would have credited too
much or too little. An illustrative simulation adds a second warning: a
leaked hint would be labelled credited under the earlier five-seed
interval/seed rules (family~D, not the primary Holm rule) and is
flagged only by an audit that records how each hint was chosen, so the
rules are not a substitute for that audit.

\subsection{Research questions and contributions}
Three questions organise the empirical sections.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{RQ1.} Does a declared control separate credit for an
  input from credit for the weights that read it?
  \item \textbf{RQ2.} How do credit decisions change with corpus,
  encoder, recipe, and control rung under the primary multiplicity rule?
  \item \textbf{RQ3.} What do availability and provenance audits catch
  that the credit rule alone does not?
\end{itemize}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{C1 (protocol; RQ1).} Three control rungs, three families,
  three outcomes under a one-sided paired credit test at
  $\tau{=}{+}2.0\times 10^{-3}$ on pooled held-out evaluation AUC with
  Holm (family~C) or Benjamini--Hochberg (family~E) adjustment, an
  availability gate, and an algorithm
  for registering a new message (Section~\ref{sec:protocol},
  Algorithm~\ref{alg:register}; Table~\ref{tab:evidence-types}).
  \item \textbf{C2 (credit map; RQ2).} On two encoders, three corpora,
  and three learner folds, the primary family credits the full time
  package on XES3G5M with the recurrent tracer; the aligned gap input
  is not credited, nor is any primary ASSISTments or attention-encoder
  contrast (Section~\ref{sec:transfer-ladder},
  Table~\ref{tab:credit-primary-c}, Figure~\ref{fig:credit-forest}).
  Those labels are a reanalysis, not a test that the LSTM and attention
  effects differ.
  \item \textbf{C3 (audit; RQ3).} Membership is the only pairwise
  hyperedge diagnostic that separates a leaky set from a clean one
  (Section~\ref{sec:leakage-ext}). An illustrative hint simulation would
  be labelled credited under the earlier five-seed interval/seed rules
  (family~D) and is flagged only by provenance
  (Section~\ref{sec:leak-sim}); a Junyi onset rebuild fails L2 and is
  excluded from credit (Section~\ref{sec:junyi-onset-audit}).
\end{itemize}
The ladder attributes messages on a fixed backbone. It does not
decompose the gap from the tracer to deep knowledge tracing (DKT)
\cite{piech2015dkt} ($+28.6\times 10^{-3}$ test AUC on
XES3G5M), which also reflects architecture, optimisation, and protocol.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{Auxiliary knowledge in knowledge tracing}
Graph-based tracers propagate over concept or question--concept edges
\cite{nakagawa2019gkt,tong2020skt,yang2021gikt}, dynamic variants add temporal
graph memory \cite{dygkt2024,dgekt2024}, and hypergraph tracers group
KCs, exercises, and learners into multi-way relations
\cite{thmn2025,hgkt2025,htkt2025,psykt2024,hhskt2023}. Time has a
longer history. Nagatani et al.~\cite{nagatani2019forget} add lag and
repetition features to DKT, HawkesKT \cite{wang2021hawkeskt} models
temporal cross-effects with a Hawkes kernel, LPKT \cite{shen2021lpkt}
feeds answer and interval time into learning and forgetting gates, and
the recurrent knowledge tracing machine of Lai et
al.~\cite{lai2021rktm} enriches factorisation-based tracers with
temporal knowledge states. Badran et al.~\cite{badran2025leakage}
encode step recency (rows since a concept last appeared), which differs
from the wall-clock gap studied here. These papers evaluate components
by ablations that remove an input together with its parameters, and
report one AUC per variant. The question
here is which of their messages survives a designated control, and on
which corpus and backbone.

\subsection{Evaluation reliability and leakage in knowledge tracing}
\label{sec:related-leakage}
Gervet et al.~\cite{gervet2020deepkt} compare model families on nine
datasets and find that the leader depends on dataset size and on how
much precise temporal information matters. Schmucker et
al.~\cite{schmucker2022assessing} add groups of features, including
features beyond question--response pairs and prerequisite structure,
and report the improvement from each on four large tutoring datasets.
Kim and Kim~\cite{kim2026pktreliability} re-evaluate
attention-augmented programming KT and find that the gap to DKT shrinks
once configuration and sequence construction are controlled. Naranjo
and Stoffova~\cite{naranjo2025ktrepro} score the reproducibility of KT
models on a continuum set by documentation and experimental
transparency, with DKT as a case study. They also find that papers
reporting on ASSISTments 2009--2010 differ in learner, skill, and record
counts without disclosing their preprocessing. Our protocol takes a
reproducible run as given and asks which added message earns the gain.
The pyKT benchmark
\cite{liu2022pykt} warns that KC-expanded exports let a model copy the
label of an earlier row of the same attempt. Badran and
Preisach~\cite{badran2025labelleak} identify this label leakage, show
that it hurts most on corpora with many KCs per item, and propose model
variants that prevent it; their later work removes it in the input
embedding and adds recency embeddings \cite{badran2025leakage}. Our
evaluation closes the same channel at scoring time instead, by masking
repeat-row targets (Section~\ref{sec:clean-protocol}), so the two
approaches can give different absolute AUCs on the same corpus. The pairwise audit that we reuse
\cite{daominh2026p0} bounds graph-mediated leakage of prerequisite and
similarity edges under train-only construction. Outside KT, Kapoor and
Narayanan~\cite{kapoor2023leakage} give a taxonomy of leakage, and
Bouthillier et al.~\cite{bouthillier2021variance} show that variance
from data sampling, initialisation, and hyperparameter choice markedly
affects benchmark conclusions.

These studies re-evaluate whole models or diagnose one leakage channel.
In ACM Digital Library, IEEE Xplore, Scopus, arXiv, and Google Scholar,
searched on 7~October~2026, we did not find a protocol that applies
leakage diagnostics at the hyperedge level and assigns credit to a
message by control rung across corpora and backbones. The query terms
were ``hypergraph knowledge tracing'', ``leakage audit'', ``graph
provenance'', ``KT label leakage'', ``question-level evaluation'', and
``controlled attribution''. The search log is in the repository.

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:prelim}
% =============================================================================

Let $\mathcal{D}=\{(u,t,q,c,y)\}$ be a KT log with learner $u$,
timestamp $t$, item $q$, KC $c$, and correctness
$y\in\{0,1\}$. Three learner folds partition learners with split seeds
$42/43/44$, as in the pairwise audit \cite{daominh2026p0}. Within each
fold the credit runs carve an internal selection split from the training
learners only ($\mathrm{val\_frac}{=}0.1$) for early stopping and
checkpoint choice. The fold's original validation and original test
learners are held out of training and of that internal selection; primary
AUC is pooled over their union (pooled held-out evaluation AUC). The
$0.7/0.1/0.2$ ratios of the pairwise audit describe the external
partition into training, original-validation, and original-test learner
pools; they are not a second selection/evaluation split inside the credit
runs. Training seeds are a separate family that initialises models: the
credit map uses the ten seeds
$\{0,17,42,1234,2024,7,99,314,2718,3407\}$. The first five are the seeds
of an earlier five-seed map and of the contextual baselines.

\subsection{Reused train-only construction}
\label{sec:p0-selfcontained}
For each fold $f$, prerequisite and similarity edges
$E_{\mathrm{pre}}^f$ and $E_{\mathrm{sim}}^f$ are built from
training-fold interactions only and cycle-pruned to a directed acyclic
graph (DAG) \cite{daominh2026p0}. Four pairwise diagnostics are
computed on the train-only graph against held-out learners; their
symbols follow \cite{daominh2026p0}.
$\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{flag}}$ is a structural learner-overlap indicator,
and $\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{overlap}}$ is held-out pattern overlap on
retained edges. $|\rho|$ is the magnitude of the Pearson correlation
between edge weight and held-out outcome. TBMR, the temporal boundary
mixing rate, is the share of
post-cutoff training mass that supports retained prerequisite
transitions; it is a within-train temporal measure, not a train/test
violation. Switching
from full-log to train-only construction shifts baseline AUC by at most
$0.003$ on XES3G5M, ASSISTments~2012, and Junyi \cite{daominh2026p0}.
That code is fixed at commit \texttt{5e3d6a0}.

A hyperedge $h=(\kappa,\{(\tau_i,e_i)\},f)$ has a kind $\kappa$
(path-derived concept group, session, discussion thread, or teacher
intervention), typed members $(\tau_i,e_i)$, and a fold $f$.
Path-derived concept groups are the member sets of bounded-length paths
on audited $E_{\mathrm{pre}}^f$ (\ref{app:hyperedge-construction}).
They are audited and used in diagnostic arms; they are not inputs of
the credited arms.

\subsection{Reference tracer}
\label{sec:encoding}
The reference backbone is a long short-term memory (LSTM) tracer with
train-only question--KC
incidence and an optional linear gap branch; concept-level hypergraph
convolution is off. Let $A\in\mathbb{R}^{N_q\times N_c}$ be the
train-only question--KC incidence, row-normalised to $\bar{A}$. Each
item $q$ has an embedding $e_q$ and each KC $c$ an embedding $e_c$. One
propagation step, in the spirit of GIKT \cite{yang2021gikt}, is
\begin{equation}
\tilde{e}_q = \tanh\bigl(W(e_q + \bar{A}_{q,:}E_c)\bigr),
\label{eq:qkc}
\end{equation}
with one shared $W$ on the sum, followed by the LSTM input projection.
The capacity-matched control keeps the same pathway and replaces $\bar{A}$
by zeros, so the incidence result concerns this shared-sum fusion, not
question--KC relations under a richer gate. Interaction embeddings of
$(c_t,y_t)$ and Rasch-style item parameters
\cite{ghosh2020akt,liu2023simplekt} feed the LSTM, and step
$t{+}1$ is scored by a query at the known identity of the next item and
KC. Table~\ref{tab:terms} names the two timed tracers (QKC-T and
Zero$+\Delta t$); implementation aliases are listed in
Table~\ref{tab:aliases}.

\paragraph{Time package}
The elapsed-time package is one linear map of the log gap, because a
single added branch has to be comparable across corpora. The map is
$f(x)=Wx+b$ of $x=\log(1+\Delta t)$
($\mathrm{Linear}(1,128)$ with bias) added on the LSTM input, the
next-step query, or both. XES3G5M records the start time of answering
in milliseconds and no end time \cite{liu2023xes3g5m}. Rows are sorted
by learner, timestamp, and item, and $\Delta t=\max(t_i-t_{i-1},0)$
seconds between consecutive rows of the same learner. A learner's first
row and later KC-rows of the same attempt have $\Delta t{=}0$, so the
branch can encode elapsed time and attempt-boundary structure; a raw
gap of $0$ still yields $f(0)=b$. The gap includes time on the previous
item plus idle time and is not the duration of the scored item. We
score at attempt onset: the prediction for item $t{+}1$ is produced when
the learner opens it, so the query-side gap has elapsed and the item's
identity is part of the request. The query never sees the scored
step's response. This holds for start timestamps, which XES3G5M and
ASSISTments~2012 store. Junyi stores completion times, so the gap of
row $t{+}1$ includes the time the learner spent on item $t{+}1$, and the
query-side gap carries information about the response being scored.
Junyi results with a query-side gap are therefore not attempt-onset
results; the recurrent-side gap at step $t$ covers only item $t$, whose
response is already an input, and has no such look-ahead
(Table~\ref{tab:junyi-history}).

\subsection{Second encoder}
\label{sec:second-encoder}
A message is credited inside one backbone, so the second backbone must
accept the same time branch and query; the pyKT implementations of simpleKT
and AKT take no gap input. The second backbone is therefore built
inside the tracer: a two-layer causal
Transformer encoder (four heads, feed-forward width twice the hidden
size, sinusoidal positions, causal mask) replaces the LSTM, and the
inputs, the time branch, the query, and the readout are unchanged. It
uses the same reference recipe as the LSTM and was not tuned
separately.

\subsection{Hyperedge membership audit}
\label{sec:leakage-ext}
The pairwise diagnostics of Section~\ref{sec:p0-selfcontained} are
applied to the pairwise projection of a hyperedge set; when the
projection exceeds $10^5$ pairs, TBMR and $|\rho|$ use a seed-42
sample of $10^5$ pairs. The audit adds a group-membership leak rate:
the share of hyperedges with at least one member whose supporting
interactions include a held-out learner. Two further classes,
cross-modal and intervention-timing leakage, need logs that the public
corpora do not have (\ref{app:hyperedge-construction}); they are
simulated in Section~\ref{sec:leak-sim}.

To test whether the audit can register leakage, a full-log positive
control builds chain hyperedges from $E_{\mathrm{pre}}$ pooled before
the split, with a concept-to-interaction map from the whole log.
Table~\ref{tab:leakage-measured} shows the result on XES3G5M fold~0.
The membership leak rate moves from $0$ to $1$. TBMR moves from $0.7341$
to $0.7357$ and does not separate the arms. $|\rho|$ is degenerate
rather than zero, because the projection gives every pair weight $1$.
Of the inherited diagnostics, only membership works at this layer.

The full-log control is close to a construction check, since a full-log
member map touches held-out learners by design. A graded control keeps
the $446{,}305$ train-only hyperedges fixed and adds a random fraction
$p$ of the $5{,}420$ held-out learners to the member map (seed~42). One
held-out learner ($p{=}10^{-4}$) raises the membership leak rate from
$0$ to $0.964$, although held-out interactions are only $0.007\%$ of the
support of the hyperedges; five learners raise it to $0.99999$, and
$54$ learners ($p{=}0.01$) to $1$. The held-out support share grows
with $p$ ($0.0004$, $0.0044$, $0.041$, and $0.30$ at $p{=}0.001$,
$0.01$, $0.1$, and $1$), and weighting each hyperedge by its support
gives the same values to two significant figures
(Table~\ref{tab:leakage-measured}). The membership rate therefore
detects contamination down to a single learner but saturates at once,
so it is a detector and not a measure of how much held-out data
entered; the support share gives the amount. The per-learner counts
are in the repository.

\subsection{Datasets}
\label{sec:reuse}
Three public corpora carry the credit map, and the timestamp unit of
each export changes what a time message can mean.
XES3G5M \cite{liu2023xes3g5m} is the reference corpus: 18{,}066
learners, 7{,}652 items, 865~KCs, and 5{,}549{,}635 attempts, which
expand to 6{,}413{,}353 KC-level rows. ASSISTments~2012
\cite{feng2009assist} and Junyi \cite{chang2015junyi}, as
processed by the pairwise audit \cite{daominh2026p0}, are the transfer
corpora. On ASSISTments~2012 the train-only incidence links $51{,}321$
of $53{,}086$ items (mean degree $0.97$). Junyi has about 25~million
interactions and items map one-to-one to KCs, so no incidence contrast
is run there; its expert prerequisite DAG is also used for a
descriptive overlap check (\ref{app:junyi-gt}). FoundationalASSIST
\cite{foundationalassist2026} supplies synchronised hints for
single-seed diagnostics (\ref{app:foundational-assist}).

Table~\ref{tab:gap-descriptives} describes the gap on each corpus. Two
properties of the processed corpora bear on the time message. In the
ASSISTments~2012 export of the pairwise audit, start times are stored
at a resolution of $1{,}000$\,s, so $82.7\%$ of non-first rows have a
zero gap between distinct items ($82.9\%$ in
Table~\ref{tab:gap-descriptives} also counts the repeat KC-rows of one
attempt), and rows inside one $1{,}000$\,s window are ordered by item
identifier. We therefore re-export ASSISTments~2012 from the raw
file at one-second resolution with the same rows and learners; the
stored timestamps of the original export equal the new ones divided by
$1{,}000$ and rounded down. In the re-export $0.01\%$ of gaps are
zero, the median positive gap is $50$\,s, and the train-only
prerequisite edges of fold~0 overlap those of the original export with
a Jaccard index of $0.59$. The one-second export is used for every
ASSISTments row of the credit map; the original export is kept as a
resolution sensitivity check (Table~\ref{tab:credit-assist-resolution-10seed}),
and the teacher-grouping diagnostic of Section~\ref{sec:below-rule} was
run on it. Junyi records completion times (\texttt{time\_done}) in
microseconds. A gap between completion times includes the time spent
on the later item, so on Junyi the query-side gap contains the duration
of the response being scored (Section~\ref{sec:encoding}). The
tracer received $\log(1{+}\Delta t)$ in microseconds. For gaps of one
second or more this differs from the value in seconds by an offset
between $13.1$ and $\ln 10^{6}\approx 13.8$, close to a constant that
the bias of the linear map can absorb; zero gaps, $0.004\%$ of Junyi
rows, are the exception.

\subsection{Recipes and seeds}
\label{sec:credit-gate}
Every arm within a setting shares one recipe. The reference recipe has
no concept or session hypergraph, batch~16, at most 30~epochs, and early
stopping with patience~5 on validation AUC. It is used on XES3G5M (both
encoders), on the attention encoder on the ASSISTments one-second
export (exploratory), on all Junyi learners, and on a second
ASSISTments~2012 ladder. A first ASSISTments~2012 ladder and a Junyi
subsample (first $50{,}000$ training and evaluation learners) keep their
corpus configurations (concept-prerequisite hypergraph on, batch~32,
10~epochs), so for those rows corpus and recipe change together. Every
credit contrast uses the ten training seeds of Section~\ref{sec:prelim}.
Every setting except the $1{,}000$\,s ASSISTments export is run on
learner folds~0, 1, and~2. Folds~1 and~2 repeat the package and the
aligned gap against T-zero; on the attention encoder and on
ASSISTments~2012 under its corpus configuration they repeat every
fold-0 contrast. The seed-42 runs of the XES3G5M reference arms were
retrained for this map and reproduced the stored validation and test
AUCs exactly. Primary credit outcomes use pooled held-out evaluation
AUC; validation
AUC selects the checkpoint and is reported beside the primary map. A
matched optimiser grid for the tracer on XES3G5M fold~0
(Section~\ref{sec:baselines-reuse}) is contextual and outside the credit
map. The run plan in the repository schedules the map, its five-seed
predecessor, the leakage simulation, the attention-ASSISTments
exploratory arms, and the tracer grid; each training run uses one
NVIDIA RTX~3090 (24\,GB). The ladder alone has $1{,}198$ finished
training stamps; $302$ of them record wall time ($185$ GPU-hours), and
imputing that mean duration to the remainder gives about $730$
GPU-hours for the ladder on one GPU. The first five seeds of the
XES3G5M fold-0 arms and the contextual baselines were trained
separately.

\subsection{Evaluation protocol}
\label{sec:clean-protocol}
The pyKT KC-level export expands a multi-KC question into consecutive
rows that share timestamp and answer, so many evaluation positions can
be answered by copying the input label \cite{liu2022pykt}. We mask the
targets of those repeat rows in our models and in the retrained pyKT
baselines, use chunked windows of length $L{=}400$, and report test-only
AUC on the remaining KC-rows. This is not pyKT's question-level
evaluator: KC scores of one attempt are not fused. Scoring every
exported row instead of the masked remainder raises the tracer's AUC by
$0.0101$ on fold~0 (\ref{app:protocol-residual}). Chunk-boundary and
event-identity checks are in \ref{app:protocol-detail}.

\subsection{Contextual baselines}
\label{sec:baselines-reuse}
DKT \cite{piech2015dkt}, AKT \cite{ghosh2020akt}, \textit{simpleKT}
\cite{liu2023simplekt}, GIKT \cite{yang2021gikt}, and DGEKT
\cite{dgekt2024} are retrained with their pyKT implementations
under the same masked, chunked protocol. GIKT shares the tracer's
training envelope (batch~16, 30~epochs, patience~5); the others keep
their inherited defaults with $L$ raised to 400 (\ref{app:hparams}).
AKT and \textit{simpleKT} are also tuned on a grid of $12$
configurations each (learning rate $\{10^{-3},3{\times}10^{-4}\}$,
dropout $\{0.05,0.10,0.20\}$, model width $\{128,256\}$), selected on
seed-42 validation AUC and then trained on the five baseline seeds; the
inherited rows are kept beside the tuned ones. The tracer itself is
retuned on a matched $12$-point grid on the XES3G5M fold-0
zero-incidence time package (learning rate
$\{10^{-3},3{\times}10^{-4}\}$, dropout $\{0.1,0.2,0.3\}$, width
$\{128,256\}$), selected on seed-42 validation AUC and then trained on
seeds $\{0,17,1234,2024\}$, as fixed in the repository before those
runs. The selected vector is learning rate $10^{-3}$, dropout $0.3$
(the grid edge), width $128$; its four-seed test-only mean is
$0.8304{\pm}0.0002$, against $0.8295{\pm}0.0001$ for the reference
recipe on five seeds (Table~\ref{tab:auc-xes3g5m}). That lift does not
enter the credit map, and the credit map was not rerun under the
selected vector.
The tracer architecture was selected on a validation ablation ladder,
so the comparison remains contextual. The protocol credits messages
inside one backbone; baseline rows locate that backbone and are not
part of the credit map. DyGKT \cite{dygkt2024}, the nearest
temporal-graph tracer, stays among the models not run under this
evaluator in this study; omitting it does not change any credit outcome.

\subsection{Synthetic leakage simulation}
\label{sec:leak-sim}
\paragraph{RQ3}
\paragraph{Design}
The public corpora have no forum, video, or teacher logs, so the
cross-modal and intervention-timing classes of
Table~\ref{tab:leakage-taxonomy} cannot be observed on them. Both were
simulated on XES3G5M fold~0 with the design fixed in the repository
before those runs. Each class adds one per-row scalar through one
$\mathrm{Linear}(1,128)$ branch to the credited zero-incidence package.
The scalar comes in a leaky form and a clean control, which share the
branch and the five seeds of the earlier map. The cross-modal scalar
stands in for a content embedding pooled after the cutoff. It is the
mean correctness of the item over all later interactions in the full
log, including held-out learners, shrunk toward the training mean with
ten pseudo-counts, and it enters the next-step query. Its clean control
uses only earlier interactions of training learners. The
intervention-timing scalar is a hint flag at step $t$ that enters the
recurrent input at $t$. In the leaky form it is drawn with probability
$0.10$ if the response at $t{+}1$ is wrong and $0.03$ if it is right;
in the clean control it is drawn independently at the same
training-split rate ($4.4\%$). Neither scalar is evidence about a
public corpus.

\paragraph{Result}
The earlier five-seed interval and seed rules alone do not catch the
leaked hint (Table~\ref{tab:leak-simulation}). It raises validation AUC
by $+5.40$ (95\% interval $[+5.25,+5.55]$) and test-only AUC by $+5.18$,
and every one of those five seeds clears $\tau$, so the leaky arm
compared with its clean control would be labelled credited under that
earlier rule. That calculation is a family~D diagnostic; it is not the
primary ten-seed Holm procedure of Section~\ref{sec:protocol-threshold}. The cross-modal scalar adds
nothing measurable ($-0.09$ validation, $-0.05$ test). A plausible
reading, which we did not test, is that the item embedding already
carries item difficulty, so a future item mean adds little; a richer
content channel could behave differently. The audit statistics separate
both leaky arms from their clean controls ($1$ against $0$), and under graded
contamination they grow roughly linearly with the leaky fraction, so
unlike the membership rate they measure the amount. The selection-after-outcome
rate is known only because the simulation records how each hint was
chosen. A data-only check that a real audit could run, the residual
$y_{t+1}-p_{t+1}$ of the clean-control model, is about $0.19$ lower on
hinted than on unhinted training positions on every seed: hinted steps
are followed by more errors than the model expects. A hint chosen
because the learner was about to fail produces that sign, but so could
a hint that changes learning, so the check flags a dependence without
proving leakage.

\subsection{Junyi attempt-onset availability audit}
\label{sec:junyi-onset-audit}
An attempt-onset Junyi gap was admitted to credit only if
$\mathrm{onset}(t)=\mathrm{time\_done}(t)-\mathrm{duration}(t)$ is
available when item $t$ opens (repository note; Data Availability
Statement). The duration field $\texttt{time\_taken}$ is present on
$99.996\%$ of training rows, so the rebuild was launched. A CPU check
fixed before it ran then tested whether that onset is a real start time
or a post-response construct.

On learner fold~0 training rows, the single-feature AUC of the onset
gap for correctness is $0.545$, while the completion-time gap (family~D)
has AUC $0.335$. The fixed L1 rule
($|A_{\mathrm{onset}}-0.5|>|A_{\mathrm{done}}-0.5|$) therefore does not
fire. L2 does: among scored rows with $\texttt{count\_attempts}>1$,
$60.5\%$ have a rebuilt onset before the previous completion of the
same learner, against $3.0\%$ among single-attempt rows (margin
$57$ points; threshold $10$). Multi-attempt rows are almost always
first-response errors ($46$ correct out of $2.47$ million), so an onset
that moves with retries is not a valid attempt-onset feature for credit:
it fails the availability audit and is treated as a leakage diagnostic.
Zero onset
gaps are likewise concentrated on retries ($28.6\%$ against $0.7\%$).
Gains of about $+65\times 10^{-3}$ validation AUC on the finished onset
seeds are therefore treated as leakage. Every Junyi onset contrast
leaves family~C and is reported in family~D; further onset experiments
were discontinued after the audit, and history-only arms are used for
the availability check (\ref{app:junyi-onset}).

\subsection{What the credit map says}
\label{sec:auc-tradeoff}
The protocol answers which message survived which control, not whether
a hypergraph network beats GIKT. Four findings follow from
Table~\ref{tab:credit-primary-c}, and a fifth from the simulation and
the Junyi audits (Table~\ref{tab:junyi-history},
Section~\ref{sec:junyi-onset-audit}).

First, the rung matters. On XES3G5M the time message is credited only
as a package. Holding the map fixed and replacing the gap leaves a gain
that is real on every seed and not credited after adjustment (means
near $\tau$), and a map fed only zeros already accounts for $+0.70$. A
package-level ablation, which is how most auxiliary components are
evaluated, would have credited the gap input with more than the input
supports. Because $\tau$ sits between the package means
($\approx{+}2.6\times 10^{-3}$) and the aligned-gap means
($\approx{+}2.0\times 10^{-3}$), that separation is also sensitive to the
operating threshold (Table~\ref{tab:credit-primary-tau}).

Second, credit decisions differ by setting, but effect sizes need not.
Under the primary rule the same message is credited as a package on
XES3G5M with the LSTM on all three learner folds, and on no
primary-family ASSISTments or attention-encoder contrast. Those two
labels are not a test that the LSTM and attention effects differ.
Compare estimates, not labels alone: XES3G5M package means are about
$+2.6$ to $+2.9\times 10^{-3}$ with seed SD near $0.28\times 10^{-3}$,
while ASSISTments reference-recipe packages are about $+2.5$ with seed
SD near $0.7\times 10^{-3}$ (Table~\ref{tab:credit-primary-c}). The C/P
difference reflects that variance and Holm over $29$ contrasts more than
a demonstrated setting$\times$effect interaction
(Tables~\ref{tab:credit-primary-mde},~\ref{tab:credit-primary-var}). An
exploratory attention package on the ASSISTments one-second export
(Table~\ref{tab:attn-assist}) stays in the same
positive-not-credited band as the XES3G5M attention rows.
Junyi completion-time and onset gaps are family~D; history-only Junyi
is exploratory and not credited. This agrees with the observation that
the leading KT model family depends on the dataset and on how much
temporal information matters \cite{gervet2020deepkt}. The gap
descriptives in Table~\ref{tab:gap-descriptives} show where the gap
carries the most signal. Scored alone, $\log(1{+}\Delta t)$ separates
correct from incorrect responses far more on Junyi (AUC $0.335$, that
is $0.665$ with the sign reversed) than on XES3G5M ($0.468$) or the
one-second ASSISTments export ($0.458$). Much of that separation is
expected, because the Junyi completion gap includes the time spent on
the scored item; the history-only check shows the attempt-onset signal
is smaller and not credited. On the $1{,}000$\,s export the gap AUC is
$0.492$ and the package is not credited. Neither descriptive explains
the attention results, since those comparisons hold the corpus fixed; a
recurrent state may use an additive gap input more readily than
attention does. The ladder records these credit-label differences; it
does not identify their cause.

Third, preprocessing is part of the setting. The two ASSISTments
exports have the same rows and learners and differ only in timestamp
resolution. On the $1{,}000$\,s export and on the one-second re-export,
the package is not credited under the primary rule; the re-export
still raises the no-time arm by about $0.013$ in validation AUC. Coarse
timestamps remove most within-session gaps and reorder rows inside each
window, so a time message evaluated on such an export is evaluated on a
different signal. What a timestamp marks matters as much as its
resolution. On Junyi, where it marks completion, the query-side gap
carries the scored item's duration and is family~D; fed to the
recurrent input only, the same gap is positive and not credited. A
rebuilt onset that moves with post-response retries fails the
availability audit. The credit record of a time message should therefore
state the timestamp resolution, its meaning (start or completion), and
the row order it was evaluated on.

Fourth, structural messages fail only in the forms and backbones
tested. Question--KC incidence is a
near-null under the shared-sum fusion on XES3G5M and is
positive, not credited, on ASSISTments~2012. Hypergraph convolution,
teacher grouping, and exponential forgetting do not reach the threshold
in the configurations run. These are statements about forms on
backbones, not about the value of structural knowledge in general.

Fifth, the credit rules do not detect leakage. The synthetic hint
simulation is an illustration, not a corpus finding: a flag chosen with
knowledge of the next response gains $+5.18\times 10^{-3}$ test AUC
($+5.40$ on the selection split), more than twice $\tau$, on every one
of five seeds under the earlier interval/seed rules
(Table~\ref{tab:leak-simulation}; family~D, not the primary Holm
procedure). Those rules ask whether a held-out gain
clears $\tau$; a leaked input can. In the simulation
the audit separates the leaky arm only because it records how each hint
was chosen. The data-only residual check flags a dependence but cannot
tell leakage from a hint that changes learning. A leak of this class is
therefore caught only if the provenance of each hint is logged and
audited before credit is assessed, which is why the protocol places the
audit first. Junyi gives two cases on real data: a completion-time
query gap (family~D by construction) and an onset rebuild that fails
L2 when overlap concentrates on multi-attempt rows
(Section~\ref{sec:junyi-onset-audit}). We add the timestamp-semantics
class to the leakage taxonomy (Table~\ref{tab:leakage-taxonomy}); its
diagnostic is the fourth practice below.

\subsection{Implications for building knowledge tracing systems}
For a knowledge engineer adding auxiliary knowledge to a tracer, the
results suggest five practices.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Build the knowledge from training learners only, run the
  membership audit before training, and log how each hint-like input
  was chosen, because the pairwise leakage statistics do not register
  hyperedge contamination and a leaked input can pass the credit rules.
  \item Declare the rung and the control before the run; if only a
  package control is available, report the credit as a package credit
  and the parameter count with it.
  \item Report outcomes per corpus, backbone, recipe, and learner fold,
  and treat a credited label on one setting as a hypothesis on the next;
  similar mean gains can receive different labels under family-wise
  adjustment.
  \item Check the timestamp resolution, row order, and meaning of every
  export before evaluating a time message: the smallest positive gap,
  the share of zero gaps, and whether a timestamp marks the start or the
  completion of an attempt. If it marks completion, rerun the credit
  with the gap in the recurrent history only.
  \item Keep positive-not-credited results in the record, and report
  the earlier interval/seed outcomes beside the primary ones when they
  disagree, since near $\tau$ the operating rule can change a cell
  without changing the sign of the effect.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Threats to validity and limitations}
\label{sec:threats}
\label{sec:limitations}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Folds and rules.} Every setting is run on three learner
  folds except the $1{,}000$\,s ASSISTments export, which is a fold-0
  sensitivity check. On XES3G5M with the LSTM and on ASSISTments under
  the reference recipe, folds~1 and~2 repeat only the package and the
  aligned gap against T-zero. The primary rule, families, and pooled
  held-out evaluation-AUC metric were fixed in the repository after the
  ten-seed runs existed; that note re-analyses those runs and says so
  (Data Availability Statement). The threshold $\tau$ remains an
  operating choice, and several package means sit near it
  (\ref{app:ten-seed-stats}). Two family~C ASSISTments rows change
  relative to the earlier test-interval rule
  (Table~\ref{tab:credit-primary-changes}). Credit labels are decisions
  under that rule; we do not report a formal setting$\times$effect
  interaction test.
  \item \textbf{Selection versus evaluation.} Checkpoints are chosen on
  an internal selection split carved from training learners; primary
  outcomes use pooled held-out evaluation AUC on the union of the fold's
  original validation and original test learners. Inference for credit
  is over training seeds, not resampled learners. A learner-clustered
  bootstrap of every family~C pair is in
  Table~\ref{tab:learner-bootstrap} (full 29 rows in
  \ref{app:bootstrap}); those intervals do not decide credit. The
  earlier validation-AUC interval/seed map is in
  \ref{app:ten-seed-stats}.
  \item \textbf{Time versus boundary.} Zero gaps also mark later
  KC-rows of one attempt and learner starts, so the credited package
  is attempt-context timing and boundary information, not a pure
  elapsed-time effect. The indicator $1[\Delta t{=}0]$ alone accounts
  for about $60\%$ of the aligned-gap mean over T-zero on this XES3G5M
  export; that share is descriptive for the KC-expanded rows used here.
  The XES3G5M schema has no end time, and a tracer that scores before
  the learner opens the next item must drop the query-side gap; the
  credited package is therefore not a claim about next-item selection
  (Table~\ref{tab:availability}). A confirmatory three-arm comparison
  on one row per attempt, with matched parameter count on the two
  branched arms, is locked in the 5~November~2026 note and is not
  reported until those runs exist.
  \item \textbf{Timestamp handling.} The ASSISTments~2012 map uses a
  one-second re-export of the pairwise-audit corpus, whose reused
  pairwise results were computed on the $1{,}000$\,s export; the
  teacher-grouping diagnostic was also run on the $1{,}000$\,s export.
  Junyi stores completion times, so its query-side gap includes the
  time spent on the scored item and is not available at attempt
  onset. History-only gaps (available at onset) were run on the
  $50{,}000$-learner subsample and on all-learner folds~0--2 with ten
  seeds each and remain exploratory (family~E), not credited.
  Candidate onset timestamps were reconstructed from completion times
  and recorded durations ($\texttt{time\_done}$ minus
  $\texttt{time\_taken}$). The reconstruction failed the availability
  audit; completed onset runs are therefore reported only as leakage
  diagnostics, and further onset experiments were discontinued.
  The Junyi gap also entered the tracer in microseconds
  (Section~\ref{sec:reuse}). For gaps of one second or more the unit
  change is hypothesised to be a near-constant offset absorbed by the
  linear-map bias; we did not rerun Junyi in seconds, so that credit
  invariance is untested.
  \item \textbf{Tracer retune.} The credit map uses the reference
  tracer configuration. A later $12$-point grid raised fold-0 test AUC
  to $0.8304{\pm}0.0002$ with dropout $0.3$ at the grid edge
  (Section~\ref{sec:baselines-reuse}); that configuration was not rerun
  through the ladder, so credit under it remains untested.
  \item \textbf{Second encoder.} The attention encoder is a swap inside
  the tracer under the LSTM recipe, not simpleKT or AKT as published.
  On XES3G5M it is in family~C; on ASSISTments it is exploratory under
  the same recipe (Table~\ref{tab:attn-assist}). Its larger seed
  variance may reflect less tuning; both outcomes are statements about
  this configuration. A credit on the LSTM package and a non-credit on
  the attention package is not a test that the two effects differ.
  \item \textbf{Fusion and forms.} The incidence null is for the shared
  $W$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:qkc}; richer fusion, gating, or untied maps were
  not run. One exponential decay form was tested; power-law decay,
  learned half-lives, and Hawkes kernels were not.
  \item \textbf{Baselines.} AKT, \textit{simpleKT}, and the tracer
  optimiser were each tuned on a $12$-point grid selected on one seed;
  the \textit{simpleKT} and tracer optima lie at a grid edge. GIKT,
  DKT, and DGEKT keep inherited defaults apart from GIKT's training
  envelope. The tracer grid is contextual and does not enter the credit
  map. DyGKT and recent pyKT models such as sparseKT were not run under
  this protocol.
  \item \textbf{Audit coverage.} Discussion-thread and
  teacher-intervention hyperedges are data-blocked on public corpora.
  The cross-modal and intervention-timing classes are simulated with
  one scalar each on one corpus and fold, with five seeds, not observed.
  The simulation statistics use provenance that a real log may not
  record. The residual check is the only data-only test, and it does
  not separate leakage from a hint that changes learning.
\end{itemize}

% =============================================================================
\section{Conclusion and future work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% =============================================================================
We propose a credit protocol for validating auxiliary knowledge in
knowledge tracing: train-only construction, membership and availability
audits, a declared control rung, ten paired seeds, and three outcomes
under a one-sided primary rule on pooled held-out evaluation AUC, with
Holm control of family-wise error in family~C and Benjamini--Hochberg
control of false-discovery rate in family~E. The family~C map is a
reanalysis of collected ten-seed runs under a rule fixed after an
earlier interval/seed map was known; it is not a confirmatory test on
unused data. In that family, only the full time package on XES3G5M with
a recurrent tracer is credited, on all three learner folds. The aligned
gap input---the elapsed-time variable alone, with the map held
fixed---stays positive and is not credited after Holm adjustment. That
package credit is for attempt-onset scoring with a query-side gap, not
for choosing the next item before it opens. No primary ASSISTments or
attention-encoder contrast is credited; a credit on one encoder and a
non-credit on the other is not a test that the two effects differ.
Junyi completion-time and onset gaps are diagnostics only; history-only
Junyi is exploratory and not credited, so no attempt-onset gap input is
credited on any corpus. Question--concept incidence is not credited on
XES3G5M. An indicator-only input accounts for about $60\%$ of the
aligned-gap mean on the KC-expanded XES3G5M export used here, and an
ASSISTments export with $1{,}000$\,s timestamps does not credit the
package, so the export is part of the setting. Credit labels can differ
across settings even when package means are similar; a single map would
still misstate at least the rung or the Junyi availability case. In an
illustrative simulation a leaked hint gains $+5.18\times 10^{-3}$
test AUC and would be labelled credited under the earlier five-seed
interval/seed rules; that calculation is family~D, not the primary
ten-seed Holm rule, and the leak is flagged only by an audit that
records how each hint was chosen, so provenance must be logged and
audited before credit is assessed. Follow-up runs locked on
5~November~2026 (one-row-per-attempt time versus boundary; matched-budget
warnings; ten-seed leak-sim) are reported in
Section~\ref{sec:nov5-followup} and Table~\ref{tab:leak-simulation};
they do not enter family~C.
