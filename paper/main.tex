\begin{abstract}
Knowledge tracing systems are often built by adding auxiliary
knowledge, such as concept graphs or response timing, to a sequence
model, and are then judged by one area under the ROC curve. That single
number does not show which added piece earned the gain, and knowledge
built from the full log can carry held-out learners into the evaluation.
We propose a credit protocol that builds such knowledge from training
learners only, audits membership leakage, compares each component with
a control declared in advance, and assigns one of three outcomes from
paired seeds. We apply it to one elapsed-time component on three
corpora and two encoders. On XES3G5M with a recurrent tracer the
component is credited only as a package (the input together with the
weights added to read it), and a copy of those weights fed only zeros
already accounts for about a quarter of that gain; the gap input itself
is credited only on Junyi. The package is not credited under an
attention encoder or on a coarse timestamp export. On ASSISTments~2012
it is credited only when question--concept incidence is set to zero,
and only on some learner folds. Question--concept
links are not credited, so credit should be reported per corpus,
backbone, recipe, learner fold, and control level.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% =============================================================================

Knowledge tracing (KT) estimates a learner's latent mastery from an
interaction history and predicts the next response, so that a tutor
can choose the next item \cite{corbett1995bkt}. Modern tracers are built in a
knowledge-engineering fashion: knowledge is acquired from the log or
from experts, represented as question--concept incidence, prerequisite
graphs, hyperedges, or timing features, and injected into a sequence
model \cite{yang2021gikt,nakagawa2019gkt,shen2021lpkt}. The resulting
system is then usually validated by one AUC for the whole model.

That validation step is weak in two ways. First, one AUC does not say
which added piece of knowledge earned the gain. A new branch adds both
an input and parameters, and a gain of a few thousandths of AUC can only
be separated from training noise with paired seeds and a declared
threshold \cite{bouthillier2021variance}. Second, auxiliary knowledge built from
the full log can carry held-out learners into training, which is one
of the leakage types that inflate reported results across
machine-learning-based science \cite{kapoor2023leakage}. Re-evaluations
outside KT have found that published neural and graph-based gains often
shrink or vanish under fair comparison
\cite{ferraridacrema2019progress,shehzad2025gnnsbrs}. Inside KT, which
model family leads depends on the dataset \cite{gervet2020deepkt}, and
gains from attention-augmented models shrink under a controlled
protocol \cite{kim2026pktreliability}; the KC-expanded data format can
itself leak labels \cite{badran2025labelleak,liu2022pykt}. These
studies ask whether one model beats another under a fair protocol. The
question for a knowledge engineer is narrower: which piece of the
injected knowledge may be credited, against what control, and does that
decision hold on another corpus or backbone?

This paper proposes a credit protocol for that decision
(Section~\ref{sec:protocol}). Auxiliary knowledge is built from training
learners only, audited for membership leakage, and compared with a
control declared before the run. Each comparison receives one of three
outcomes.

When the protocol is applied to one elapsed-time message on two
encoders and three corpora, its verdict depends on the setting
(Table~\ref{tab:credit-transfer}). On XES3G5M with a recurrent tracer
the time package is credited, but only as a package: a map fed only
zeros already accounts for about a quarter of that gain, so a
package-level ablation overstates what the gap input contributes. The
same gap input is credited on Junyi and is not credited on XES3G5M.
On ASSISTments the time package on the tracer whose question--concept
incidence is zeroed is credited on some learner folds and not on
others, and nothing is credited under that corpus's own configuration
or on an export whose timestamps are too coarse to order events inside
a session. On XES3G5M, an attention encoder credits no time message.
A single-setting ablation would have credited too much or too little.

\subsection{Contributions}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{C1 (protocol).} Three control rungs, three outcomes,
  a seed-wise and an interval rule at $+2.0\times 10^{-3}$, and an algorithm for
  registering a new message (Section~\ref{sec:protocol},
  Algorithm~\ref{alg:register}).
  \item \textbf{C2 (credit map across settings).} On two encoders and
  three corpora, the time message is credited as an input, credited
  only as a package, or not credited. The verdict depends on the
  setting, the training recipe, the timestamp resolution, and the
  learner fold. Question--KC incidence is not credited on XES3G5M
  (Section~\ref{sec:transfer-ladder},

\end{itemize}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{Auxiliary knowledge in knowledge tracing}
Graph-based tracers propagate over concept or question--concept edges
\cite{nakagawa2019gkt,tong2020skt,yang2021gikt}, dynamic variants add temporal
graph memory \cite{dygkt2024,dgekt2024}, and hypergraph tracers group
KCs, exercises, and learners into multi-way relations
\cite{thmn2025,hgkt2025,htkt2025,psykt2024,hhskt2023}. Time has a
longer history. Nagatani et al.~\cite{nagatani2019forget} add lag and
repetition features to DKT, HawkesKT \cite{wang2021hawkeskt} models
temporal cross-effects with a Hawkes kernel, LPKT \cite{shen2021lpkt}
feeds answer and interval time into learning and forgetting gates, and
the recurrent knowledge tracing machine of Lai et
al.~\cite{lai2021rktm} enriches factorisation-based tracers with
temporal knowledge states. Badran et al.~\cite{badran2025leakage}
encode step recency (rows since a concept last appeared), which differs
from the wall-clock gap studied here. These papers evaluate components
by ablations that remove an input together with its parameters, and
report one AUC per variant. The question
here is which of their messages survives a designated control, and on
which corpus and backbone.

\subsection{Evaluation reliability and leakage in knowledge tracing}
\label{sec:related-leakage}
Gervet et al.~\cite{gervet2020deepkt} compare model families on nine
datasets and find that the leader depends on dataset size and on how
much precise temporal information matters. Schmucker et
al.~\cite{schmucker2022assessing} add groups of features, including
features beyond question--response pairs and prerequisite structure,
and report the improvement from each on four large tutoring datasets.
Kim and Kim~\cite{kim2026pktreliability} re-evaluate
attention-augmented programming KT and find that the gap to DKT shrinks
once configuration and sequence construction are controlled. Naranjo
and Stoffova~\cite{naranjo2025ktrepro} score the reproducibility of KT
models on a continuum set by documentation and experimental
transparency, with DKT as a case study. They also find that papers
reporting on ASSISTments 2009--2010 differ in learner, skill, and record
counts without disclosing their preprocessing. Our protocol takes a
reproducible run as given and asks which added message earns the gain.
The pyKT benchmark
\cite{liu2022pykt} warns that KC-expanded exports let a model copy the
label of an earlier row of the same attempt. Badran and
Preisach~\cite{badran2025labelleak} identify this label leakage, show
that it hurts most on corpora with many KCs per item, and propose model
variants that prevent it; their later work removes it in the input
embedding and adds recency embeddings \cite{badran2025leakage}. Our
evaluation closes the same channel at scoring time instead, by masking
repeat-row targets (Section~\ref{sec:clean-protocol}), so the two
approaches can give different absolute AUCs on the same corpus. The pairwise audit that we reuse
\cite{daominh2026p0} bounds graph-mediated leakage of prerequisite and
similarity edges under train-only construction. Outside KT, Kapoor and
Narayanan~\cite{kapoor2023leakage} give a taxonomy of leakage, and
Bouthillier et al.~\cite{bouthillier2021variance} show that variance
from data sampling, initialisation, and hyperparameter choice markedly
affects benchmark conclusions.

These studies re-evaluate whole models or diagnose one leakage channel.
In ACM Digital Library, IEEE Xplore, Scopus, arXiv, and Google Scholar,
searched on 7~October~2026, we did not find a protocol that applies
leakage diagnostics at the hyperedge level and assigns credit to a
message by control rung across corpora and backbones. The query terms
were ``hypergraph knowledge tracing'', ``leakage audit'', ``graph
provenance'', ``KT label leakage'', ``question-level evaluation'', and
``controlled attribution''. The search log is in the repository.

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:prelim}
% =============================================================================

Let $\mathcal{D}=\{(u,t,q,c,y)\}$ be a KT log with learner $u$,
timestamp $t$, item $q$, knowledge component (KC) $c$, and correctness
$y\in\{0,1\}$. A learner-based split
$\mathcal{F}=(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{train}},\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{val}},
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{test}})$ assigns each learner to one part (ratios
$0.7/0.1/0.2$). Three learner folds are drawn with split seeds
$42/43/44$, as in the pairwise audit \cite{daominh2026p0}. Training
seeds $\{0,17,42,1234,2024\}$ are a separate family that initialises
models; the value $42$ occurs in both by coincidence.

\subsection{Reused train-only construction}
\label{sec:p0-selfcontained}
For each fold $f$, prerequisite and similarity edges
$E_{\mathrm{pre}}^f$ and $E_{\mathrm{sim}}^f$ are built from
training-fold interactions only and cycle-pruned to a DAG
\cite{daominh2026p0}. Four pairwise diagnostics are computed on the
train-only graph against held-out learners.
$\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{flag}}$ is a structural learner-overlap indicator,
and $\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{overlap}}$ is held-out pattern overlap on
retained edges. $|\rho|$ is the magnitude of the Pearson correlation
between edge weight and held-out outcome. TBMR is the share of
post-cutoff training mass that supports retained prerequisite
transitions; it is a within-train temporal measure, not a train/test
violation. Switching
from full-log to train-only construction shifts baseline AUC by at most
$0.003$ on XES3G5M, ASSISTments~2012, and Junyi \cite{daominh2026p0}.
That code is fixed at commit \texttt{5e3d6a0}.

A hyperedge $h=(\kappa,\{(\tau_i,e_i)\},f)$ has a kind $\kappa$
(path-derived concept group, session, discussion thread, or teacher
intervention), typed members $(\tau_i,e_i)$, and a fold $f$.
Path-derived concept groups are the member sets of bounded-length paths
on audited $E_{\mathrm{pre}}^f$ (\ref{app:hyperedge-construction}).
They are audited and used in diagnostic arms; they are not inputs of
the credited arms.

\subsection{Reference tracer}
\label{sec:encoding}
The reference backbone is an LSTM tracer with train-only question--KC
incidence and an optional linear gap branch; concept-level hypergraph
convolution is off. Let $A\in\mathbb{R}^{N_q\times N_c}$ be the
train-only question--KC incidence, row-normalised to $\bar{A}$. Each
item $q$ has an embedding $e_q$ and each KC $c$ an embedding $e_c$. One
propagation step, in the spirit of GIKT \cite{yang2021gikt}, is
\begin{equation}
\tilde{e}_q = \tanh\bigl(W(e_q + \bar{A}_{q,:}E_c)\bigr),
\label{eq:qkc}
\end{equation}
with one shared $W$ on the sum, followed by the LSTM input projection.
The capacity-matched control keeps the same pathway and replaces $\bar{A}$
by zeros, so the incidence result concerns this shared-sum fusion, not
question--KC relations under a richer gate. Interaction embeddings of
$(c_t,y_t)$ and Rasch-style item parameters
\cite{ghosh2020akt,liu2023simplekt} feed the LSTM, and step
$t{+}1$ is scored by a query at the known identity of the next item and
KC. In the tables the observed-incidence time package is called QKC-T
and the zero-incidence time package is called Zero$+\Delta t$.
Implementation aliases are listed in Table~\ref{tab:aliases}.

\paragraph{Time package}
The elapsed-time package is one linear map of the log gap, because a
single added branch has to be comparable across corpora. The map is
$f(x)=Wx+b$ of $x=\log(1+\Delta t)$
($\mathrm{Linear}(1,128)$ with bias) added on the LSTM input, the
next-step query, or both. XES3G5M records the start time of answering
in milliseconds and no end time \cite{liu2023xes3g5m}. Rows are sorted
by learner, timestamp, and item, and $\Delta t=\max(t_i-t_{i-1},0)$
seconds between consecutive rows of the same learner. A learner's first
row and later KC-rows of the same attempt have $\Delta t{=}0$, so the
branch can encode elapsed time and attempt-boundary structure; a raw
gap of $0$ still yields $f(0)=b$. The gap includes time on the previous
item plus idle time and is not the duration of the scored item. We
score at attempt onset: the prediction for item $t{+}1$ is produced when
the learner opens it, so the query-side gap has elapsed and the item's
identity is part of the request. The query never sees the scored
step's response.

\subsection{Second encoder}
\label{sec:second-encoder}
A message is credited inside one backbone, so the second backbone must
accept the same time branch and query; the pyKT implementations of simpleKT
and AKT take no gap input. The second backbone is therefore built
inside the tracer: a two-layer causal
Transformer encoder (four heads, feed-forward width twice the hidden
size, sinusoidal positions, causal mask) replaces the LSTM, and the
inputs, the time branch, the query, and the readout are unchanged. It
uses the same reference recipe as the LSTM and was not tuned
separately.

\subsection{Hyperedge membership audit}
\label{sec:leakage-ext}
The pairwise diagnostics of Section~\ref{sec:p0-selfcontained} are
applied to the pairwise projection of a hyperedge set; when the
projection exceeds $10^5$ pairs, TBMR and $|\rho|$ use a seed-42
sample of $10^5$ pairs. The audit adds a group-membership leak rate:
the share of hyperedges with at least one member whose supporting
interactions include a held-out learner. Two further classes,
cross-modal and intervention-timing leakage, need logs that the public
corpora do not have (\ref{app:hyperedge-construction}).

To test whether the audit can register leakage, a full-log positive
control builds chain hyperedges from $E_{\mathrm{pre}}$ pooled before
the split, with a concept-to-interaction map from the whole log.
Table~\ref{tab:leakage-measured} shows the result on XES3G5M fold~0.
The membership leak rate moves from $0$ to $1$. TBMR moves from $0.7341$
to $0.7357$ and does not separate the arms. $|\rho|$ is degenerate
rather than zero, because the projection gives every pair weight $1$.
Of the inherited diagnostics, only membership works at this layer.

The full-log control is close to a construction check, since a full-log
member map touches held-out learners by design. A graded control keeps
the $446{,}305$ train-only hyperedges fixed and adds a random fraction
$p$ of the $5{,}420$ held-out learners to the member map (seed~42). One
held-out learner ($p{=}10^{-4}$) raises the membership leak rate from
$0$ to $0.964$, although held-out interactions are only $0.007\%$ of the
support of the hyperedges; five learners raise it to $0.99999$, and
$54$ learners ($p{=}0.01$) to $1$. The held-out support share grows
with $p$ ($0.0004$, $0.0044$, $0.041$, and $0.30$ at $p{=}0.001$,
$0.01$, $0.1$, and $1$). The membership rate therefore detects
contamination down to a single learner but saturates at once, so it is
a detector and not a measure of how much held-out data entered; the
support share gives the amount. The per-learner counts are in the
repository.

\subsection{Datasets}
\label{sec:reuse}
Three public corpora carry the credit map, and the timestamp unit of
each export changes what a time message can mean.
XES3G5M \cite{liu2023xes3g5m} is the reference corpus: 18{,}066
learners, 7{,}652 items, 865~KCs, and 5{,}549{,}635 attempts, which
expand to 6{,}413{,}353 KC-level rows. ASSISTments~2012
\cite{feng2009assist} and Junyi \cite{chang2015junyi}, as
processed by the pairwise audit \cite{daominh2026p0}, are the transfer
corpora. On ASSISTments~2012 the train-only incidence links $51{,}321$
of $53{,}086$ items (mean degree $0.97$). Junyi has about 25~million
interactions and items map one-to-one to KCs, so no incidence contrast
is run there; its expert prerequisite DAG is also used for a
descriptive overlap check (\ref{app:junyi-gt}). FoundationalASSIST
\cite{foundationalassist2026} supplies synchronised hints for
single-seed diagnostics (\ref{app:foundational-assist}).

Table~\ref{tab:gap-descriptives} describes the gap on each corpus. Two
properties of the processed corpora bear on the time message. In the
ASSISTments~2012 export of the pairwise audit, start times are stored
at a resolution of $1{,}000$\,s, so $82.7\%$ of gaps between distinct
items are zero and rows inside one $1{,}000$\,s window are ordered by
item identifier. We therefore re-export ASSISTments~2012 from the raw
file at one-second resolution with the same rows and learners; the
stored timestamps of the original export equal the new ones divided by
$1{,}000$ and rounded down. In the re-export $0.01\%$ of gaps are
zero, the median positive gap is $50$\,s, and the train-only
prerequisite edges of fold~0 overlap those of the original export with
a Jaccard index of $0.59$. The one-second export is used for every
ASSISTments row of the credit map; the original export is kept as a
resolution sensitivity check (Table~\ref{tab:credit-assist-resolution}),
and the teacher-grouping diagnostic of Section~\ref{sec:below-rule} was
run on it. Junyi records completion times in microseconds, and the
tracer received $\log(1{+}\Delta t)$ in those units. For gaps of one
second or more this differs from the value in seconds by an offset
between $13.1$ and $\ln 10^{6}\approx 13.8$, close to a constant that
the bias of the linear map can absorb; zero gaps, $0.004\%$ of Junyi
rows, are the exception.

\subsection{Recipes and seeds}
\label{sec:credit-gate}
Every arm within a setting shares one recipe. The reference recipe has
no concept or session hypergraph, batch~16, at most 30~epochs, and early
stopping with patience~5 on validation AUC. It is used on XES3G5M (both
encoders), on all Junyi learners, and on a second ASSISTments~2012
ladder. A first ASSISTments~2012 ladder and a Junyi subsample (first
$50{,}000$ training and evaluation learners) keep their corpus
configurations (concept-prerequisite hypergraph on, batch~32,
10~epochs), so for those rows corpus and recipe change together. Every
setting uses learner fold~0 and the five training seeds of
Section~\ref{sec:prelim}. On XES3G5M with the LSTM, and on
ASSISTments~2012 under the reference recipe, the package and the
aligned-gap contrast are repeated on learner folds~1 and~2.
Outcomes use validation AUC; test-only AUC is reported for context.

\subsection{Evaluation protocol}
\label{sec:clean-protocol}
The pyKT KC-level export expands a multi-KC question into consecutive
rows that share timestamp and answer, so many evaluation positions can
be answered by copying the input label \cite{liu2022pykt}. We mask the
targets of those repeat rows in our models and in the retrained pyKT
baselines, use chunked windows of length $L{=}400$, and report test-only
AUC on the remaining KC-rows. This is not pyKT's question-level
evaluator: KC scores of one attempt are not fused. Scoring every
exported row instead of the masked remainder raises the tracer's AUC by
$0.0101$ on fold~0 (\ref{app:protocol-residual}). Chunk-boundary and
event-identity checks are in \ref{app:protocol-detail}.

\subsection{Contextual baselines}
\label{sec:baselines-reuse}
DKT \cite{piech2015dkt}, AKT \cite{ghosh2020akt}, \textit{simpleKT}
\cite{liu2023simplekt}, GIKT \cite{yang2021gikt}, and DGEKT
\cite{dgekt2024} are retrained with their pyKT implementations
under the same masked, chunked protocol. GIKT shares the tracer's
training envelope (batch~16, 30~epochs, patience~5); the others keep
their inherited defaults with $L$ raised to 400 (\ref{app:hparams}).
The tracer was selected on a validation ablation ladder, whereas each
baseline used one inherited hyperparameter vector, so the comparison
favours the tracer and is contextual. The protocol credits messages
inside one backbone; baseline rows locate that backbone and are not
part of the credit map. DyGKT \cite{dygkt2024}, the nearest
temporal-graph tracer, is not among the pyKT implementations used here
and was not run, since a re-implementation would add a row of context
without changing any credit outcome.

\subsection{The credit map across settings}
\label{sec:transfer-ladder}
Table~\ref{tab:credit-transfer} is the main result. Each row pairs an on
arm and its designated control over the five seeds and applies both
rules. Every contrast in the table except XES3G5M incidence and one
ASSISTments package contrast is positive on every seed; the settings
differ in which gains reach the threshold. The two rules give the same
outcome in every row of both tables ($44$ contrasts).

\input{figures/fig_credit_heatmap}
\input{tables/table_credit_transfer}

ASSISTments rows in Table~\ref{tab:credit-transfer} use the one-second
export; the $1{,}000$\,s export is
Table~\ref{tab:credit-assist-resolution}. Rows marked Ref.\ or (all),
and every XES3G5M row, use the reference recipe; the other ASSISTments
and Junyi rows use the corpus configuration.

\paragraph{XES3G5M, LSTM tracer}
The time package is credited, and the credit rests on the package rung
alone (Table~\ref{tab:credit-transfer}). With the map held fixed, the
aligned gap input is consistent-positive against T-zero, T-misaligned,
T-shuffled, and T-boundary. Question--KC incidence is unsupported.
A no-time control that allocates the same 256 parameters and multiplies
their output by zero is also beaten on every seed, but those weights
receive no gradient, so that control checks the parameter count only.
T-shuffled removes each learner's pace as well as the alignment, and
the aligned gap beats it by about as much as it beats T-misaligned;
per-learner pace adds little, in line with a lag-1 gap correlation of
$0.08$ (Table~\ref{tab:gap-descriptives}). Of the aligned gap's gain
over T-zero, the indicator $1[\Delta t{=}0]$ alone reproduces about
$60\%$. These are differences between separately trained arms.
On learner folds~1 and~2 the package stays credited and the aligned
gap stays consistent-positive.

\paragraph{XES3G5M, attention encoder}
No time message is credited. The no-time arm reaches $0.824$, close to
the LSTM's $0.826$, and its seed standard deviation is two to three
times the LSTM value. The package credit belongs to the LSTM tracer,
not to the gap input in general.

\paragraph{ASSISTments 2012}
Under the reference recipe the zero-incidence package is
credited on folds~0 and~2 and consistent-positive on fold~1, where one
seed falls below the threshold (Table~\ref{tab:credit-transfer}). The
aligned gap is consistent-positive on all three folds. With observed
incidence the package is not credited. Incidence is consistent-positive
here, rather than the unsupported null of XES3G5M. Early stopping
selects a checkpoint from epochs 4--7 of~30, so these runs stop far
earlier than the Junyi runs, which often continue past epoch~25.
Under the corpus configuration no message is credited: with observed

\paragraph{Junyi}
On all learners under the reference recipe, both the package and the

\subsection{Reference run on XES3G5M}
\label{sec:rq1-result}
\label{sec:time-result}
\paragraph{Incidence}
Capacity-matched zeroing of the question--KC incidence gives a mean
validation difference of $-0.10{\pm}0.34$ ($0/5$; 95\% interval
$[{-}0.52,{+}0.32]$). Under the shared-sum fusion of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:qkc}, the interval bounds the mean incidence contribution
above by about $+0.3$, so we report a bounded null. Per-seed
records are in Table~\ref{tab:qkc-capacity}.

\paragraph{What the package contains}
On the zero-incidence backbone the three separately trained arms give a
ladder of mean validation differences: $+0.71$ from no time to
T-zero (95\% interval $[{+}0.53,{+}0.90]$), $+1.83$ from T-zero
to real gaps, and $+2.55$ from no time to real gaps. The first step
adds a map whose input is zero everywhere, so only its bias and the
re-optimised network can account for it. These are absolute figures
from separate arms, not shares of a causal decomposition. T-zero removes
both the gap distribution and its alignment; T-misaligned keeps each
learner's gap distribution and breaks the order, and the two means
differ by about $27\%$. Per-seed values are in Table~\ref{tab:a1-timing}.

\paragraph{Injection site}
Table~\ref{tab:a7-query-dt} varies where the package enters. Only
injection into both the LSTM and the next-step query is credited.
History-only injection, which uses only gaps already observed, is
positive on every seed and reaches the threshold on two of five (mean
$+1.93$); query-only reaches it on none (mean $+0.99$). Both are
consistent-positive (not credited). A tracer that must score before the
learner opens the next item cannot use the query-side gap and therefore
cannot claim the credited package. A two-bin split of
$\log(1{+}\Delta t)$ shows gains for short gaps ($\lesssim$18\,min,
$+2.8$) and long gaps ($+1.9$), with both intervals excluding $0$.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{What the credit map says}
\label{sec:auc-tradeoff}
The protocol answers which message survived which control, not whether
a hypergraph network beats GIKT. Four findings follow from
Tables~\ref{tab:credit-transfer} and~\ref{tab:credit-assist-resolution}.

First, the rung matters. On XES3G5M the time message is credited only
as a package. Holding the map fixed and replacing the gap leaves a gain
that is real on every seed and below the threshold, and a map fed only
zeros already accounts for $+0.71$. A package-level ablation, which
is how most auxiliary components are evaluated, would have credited
the gap input with more than the input supports.

Second, the setting matters. Under the XES3G5M recipe the same message
is credited as a package on XES3G5M on all three learner folds and
on ASSISTments~2012 on two of three folds, and as an aligned input on
Junyi. On the remaining ASSISTments fold the package is positive on
every seed and not credited. It is not credited on ASSISTments~2012 under
the corpus configuration, on XES3G5M once the recurrent encoder is
replaced by attention, or on an ASSISTments export with coarse
timestamps. This agrees with the observation that the leading KT
model family depends on the dataset and on how much temporal
information matters \cite{gervet2020deepkt}. The gap descriptives in
Table~\ref{tab:gap-descriptives} are consistent with the corpus
reading. Scored alone, $\log(1{+}\Delta t)$ separates correct from
incorrect responses far more on Junyi (AUC $0.335$, that is $0.665$
with the sign reversed) than on XES3G5M ($0.468$) or the one-second
ASSISTments export ($0.458$), and only on Junyi is the aligned gap
credited; on the $1{,}000$\,s export the gap AUC is $0.492$ and the
package is not credited. Neither descriptive explains the attention or
recipe results, since those comparisons hold the corpus fixed; a
recurrent state may use an additive gap input more readily than
attention does. The ladder records these dependences; it does not
identify their cause.

Third, preprocessing is part of the setting. The two ASSISTments
exports have the same rows and learners and differ only in timestamp
resolution. On the $1{,}000$\,s export the package is consistent-positive
(not credited); on the one-second re-export it is credited, and the
tracer gains $0.013$ in validation AUC. Coarse timestamps
remove most within-session gaps and reorder rows inside each window, so
a time message evaluated on such an export is evaluated on a different
signal. The credit record of a time message should therefore state
the timestamp resolution and the row order it was evaluated on.

Fourth, structural messages fail only in the forms and backbones
tested. Question--KC incidence is a
bounded null under the shared-sum fusion on XES3G5M and is
consistent-positive on ASSISTments~2012. Hypergraph convolution,
teacher grouping, and exponential forgetting do not reach the threshold
in the configurations run. These are statements about forms on
backbones, not about the value of structural knowledge in general.

\subsection{Implications for building knowledge tracing systems}
For a knowledge engineer adding auxiliary knowledge to a tracer, the
results suggest five practices.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Build the knowledge from training learners only and run the
  membership audit before training, because the pairwise leakage
  statistics do not register hyperedge contamination.
  \item Declare the rung and the control before the run; if only a
  package control is available, report the credit as a package credit
  and the parameter count with it.
  \item Report outcomes per corpus, backbone, recipe, and learner fold,
  because a message validated on one corpus or one fold is a hypothesis
  on the next.
  \item Check the timestamp resolution and row order of every export
  before evaluating a time message, for example by the smallest
  positive gap and the share of zero gaps.
  \item Keep consistent-positive (not credited) results in the record,
  since a gain that is positive on every seed and below the threshold
  on one corpus was credited on another here.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Threats to validity and limitations}
\label{sec:threats}
\label{sec:limitations}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Learner folds.} The package and aligned-gap contrasts
  are repeated on three learner folds for XES3G5M and for
  ASSISTments~2012 under the reference recipe. On ASSISTments the
  package is credited on folds~0 and~2 and consistent-positive on
  fold~1. The corpus configuration, the attention encoder, and Junyi
  use fold~0 only. The interval rule was fixed after the runs were scored,
  and its threshold, like the seed-rule threshold, is an operating
  choice.
  \item \textbf{Time versus boundary.} Zero gaps also mark later
  KC-rows of one attempt and learner starts, so the credited package
  is attempt-context timing and boundary information, not a pure
  elapsed-time effect. The indicator $1[\Delta t{=}0]$ alone reproduces
  about $60\%$ of the aligned-gap gain over T-zero on XES3G5M. The
  XES3G5M schema has no end time, and a tracer that scores before the
  learner opens the next item must drop the query-side gap.
  \item \textbf{Timestamp handling.} The ASSISTments~2012 map uses a
  one-second re-export of the pairwise-audit corpus, whose reused
  pairwise results were computed on the $1{,}000$\,s export; the
  teacher-grouping diagnostic was also run on the $1{,}000$\,s export.
  The Junyi gap entered the tracer in microseconds
  (Section~\ref{sec:reuse}). For gaps of one second or more the change
  is a near-constant offset that the bias of the linear map can absorb,
  so we expect the credit outcome to be unchanged; we did not rerun
  Junyi in seconds.
  \item \textbf{Second encoder.} The attention encoder is a swap inside
  the tracer under the LSTM recipe, not simpleKT or AKT as published,
  and was run on XES3G5M only. Its larger seed variance may reflect less
  tuning; the outcome for it is a statement about this configuration.
  \item \textbf{Fusion and forms.} The incidence null is for the shared
  $W$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:qkc}; richer fusion, gating, or untied maps were
  not run. One exponential decay form was tested; power-law decay,
  learned half-lives, and Hawkes kernels were not.
  \item \textbf{Baselines.} Contextual baselines inherit defaults except
  GIKT's training envelope; DyGKT and recent pyKT models such as
  sparseKT were not run under this protocol.
  \item \textbf{Audit coverage.} Discussion-thread and
  teacher-intervention hyperedges are data-blocked on public corpora,
  and the cross-modal and intervention-timing leakage classes are not
  instantiated. A hyperedge-native weighted statistic is future work.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion and future work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% =============================================================================
We propose a credit protocol for validating auxiliary knowledge in
knowledge tracing: train-only construction, a hyperedge membership
audit, a declared control rung, five paired seeds, and three outcomes
under a seed-wise and an interval rule. On XES3G5M with a recurrent
tracer, the protocol credits the time package but not the aligned gap
input, on all three learner folds. On ASSISTments~2012 under the same
recipe, the zero-incidence package is credited on two of three folds
and stays positive, but not
credited, on the third. On XES3G5M with an attention encoder the
protocol credits neither the package nor the gap input. On
ASSISTments~2012 under its own configuration it credits nothing, and on
Junyi it credits both the package and the aligned gap input.
Question--concept incidence is not credited on XES3G5M. An
indicator-only input reproduces about $60\%$ of the aligned-gap gain on
XES3G5M, and an ASSISTments export with $1{,}000$\,s timestamps does
not credit the package, so the export is part of the setting. A single
credit map would have misstated what the time message contributes in
at least one of these settings. Future work, in order: repeat the
remaining settings (the corpus configuration, the attention encoder,
and Junyi) on further learner folds; rerun the pairwise audit on the
one-second ASSISTments export; run DyGKT and recent pyKT models under this
evaluator; and develop a hyperedge-native leakage statistic that
measures the amount of contamination rather than detecting it.
